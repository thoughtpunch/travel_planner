## Why go at all — and why one day is the right size

"La Grassa," the Fat One, is where tortellini and tagliatelle al ragù were invented, roofed by
~40 km of UNESCO porticoes so it can rain and you never get wet. From the Florence base it's
37 minutes each way, which makes it cheaper and easier as a day out than it ever was as a stop.

- **One job: eat.** Pick the meals, not a checklist. Bologna eats best at **lunch**, which is
  exactly the window a day trip gives you.
- **Everything is walkable from Centrale** — pasta class, Quadrilatero, the towers, the porticoes,
  Palazzo Poggi. No car, no transfers, no luggage.
- **It fits Dan's rhythm.** He works **16:00–23:00** Italy time Mon–Fri, so a morning train up
  and a mid-afternoon train back gets him home before the calls start. Or go on a weekend day and
  the clock stops mattering.
- **No dead travel days.** As an overnight it cost two half-days of hauling bags; as a day trip
  it costs a train ticket.

## The day — shortlist, food first

1. **Hands-on fresh-pasta class** — hand-roll the sfoglia, fold tortellini and tagliatelle, a
   station for every kid. La Vecchia Scuola Bolognese, or a home class through Cesarine. Small and
   sells out — reserve well before the Florence week starts.
2. **Quadrilatero market food crawl** — the medieval market grid off Piazza Maggiore: cured meats
   and tortelloni at Tamburini, a stand-up lunch in the Mercato di Mezzo, a glass at Osteria del
   Sole. The meal you'll talk about later.
3. **The Two Towers — climb Asinelli** — 498 wooden steps up the leaning tower for the best view
   over the red rooftops. Timed slots, small — book online ahead. (Age 8+ for the climb.)
4. **The porticoes** — a UNESCO World Heritage site. The longest, **Portico di San Luca**, climbs
   666 arches to a hilltop basilica; walk it, or ride the San Luca Express up and stroll down.
   Note this one eats a couple of hours — it competes with the museums, it doesn't stack with them.
5. **The feast — ragù, tortellini in brodo, mortadella** — the holy trinity done right: tagliatelle
   al ragù (never "spaghetti bolognese," which doesn't exist here), tortellini in brodo, real
   mortadella. On a day trip this is the **big sit-down lunch**, not a dinner — book one good
   osteria for it.
6. **Palazzo Poggi** — the university's wunderkammer: 18th-century anatomical wax models, obstetric
   teaching figures, ship models and natural-history oddities in a frescoed palace. Pure
   Escher/Voynich energy.
7. **Archiginnasio + Anatomical Theatre** — the old university hall crusted with carved student
   coats-of-arms, hiding a 1637 wooden dissection theatre watched over by skinned wooden figures.
8. **Gelato + Santo Stefano's "seven churches"** — a top cone, then the maze of seven interlinked
   churches and courtyards. Quiet, strange, free — a good last stop before the train.

**Realistic load for one day:** the pasta class *or* the San Luca portico walk, plus the
Quadrilatero, plus the big lunch, plus one museum. Trying for more is how a day trip turns into a
bad day.

## Offbeat & hidden gems

All of these are free and within a walk of the centre, so they slot into gaps without planning.

- **La Finestrella di Via Piella** — a shuttered window opening onto a hidden canal, Bologna's
  "Little Venice." Push the shutter and the street becomes Venice. (All ages · free · Via Piella 16)
- **The whispering telephone** — under the Voltone del Podestà, two people at opposite diagonal
  corners whisper into the stone and hear each other across the noisy square. (All ages · free ·
  Piazza Maggiore)
- **Neptune's not-a-finger** — from the exact spot by Sala Borsa, the bronze Neptune's thumb turns
  into something ruder. A 450-year-old visual dirty joke. (Teens + adults · free · Piazza del Nettuno)
- **The three arrows of Corte Isolani** — three ancient wooden arrows lodged in a medieval portico
  ceiling, with a grisly legend. (All ages · free · Strada Maggiore 19)
- **Cassini's sundial in San Petronio** — a 67 m brass meridian across the floor; a pinhole 27 m up
  drops a disc of sunlight at solar noon. Time this one for **midday** — it's the one gem with a
  clock on it. The half-bare-brick façade has a jealous-Pope legend attached. (All ages · free ·
  Piazza Maggiore)
- **Museo per la Memoria di Ustica** — a hushed hall in Bolognina rebuilt around the wreckage of a
  DC-9 downed in 1980, wrapped in Boltanski's installation of 81 lights and 81 mirrors. Devastating
  and unforgettable — but it only opens **Fri–Sun 17:00–20:00**, so it fits a day trip only if you
  stay late, which conflicts with Dan's work block. Weekend-only in practice. (Teens + adults · free)
- **Gelato Museum Carpigiani** — the world's first gelato museum, ~20 min out at Anzola (bus 87).
  Free entry, paid workshops booked ahead. Grey's whole trip in one building — but it's out of town,
  so it's an either/or against the city centre.
- **The tortellino & Venus's navel** — legend says the pasta ring was modelled on Venus's navel
  spied through a keyhole. Order tortellini in brodo, know the story, settle the family debate
  after the pasta class.

## Optional add-ons — each is a second day, not a bolt-on

- **Motor Valley — for Jude.** Ducati is in-city; **Ferrari and Lamborghini** sit out toward
  Modena. The site's honest read: a factory day is really its own outing, so make it a **dedicated
  second Bologna day trip** rather than trying to squeeze it in next to the food run.
- **A Parmigiano + balsamic morning toward Modena** — half an hour further by train: Parmigiano-
  Reggiano made at dawn, barrel-aged balsamic in family attics, Bottura's home city. The grown-up
  food pilgrimage of the region — and a different day than the pasta-class day.
- **Grand Tour Italia food park** (the old FICO, edge of town) — pizza and gelato made in front of
  you. Re-check opening days before committing a morning to it.

## What to book ahead

1. **Frecciarossa Firenze S.M.N. → Bologna Centrale and back** — advance fares are much cheaper
   than the ~€30–50pp walk-up. Use the family discount (split the six into two bookings, each
   carrying an under-15). If the Freccia fare is high for your date, Regionale is the flat-fare
   fallback.
2. **Fresh-pasta class** — small and sells out; reserve as soon as the Florence dates are firm.
3. **Asinelli Tower timed slot** — book online.
4. **One proper osteria lunch** — call a week or two ahead.

Nothing else. **No lodging to book** — that's the whole point of the change.

## What's tricky about a Bologna day trip

- **Pick the date around Dan's work block.** Mon–Fri he's on calls **16:00–23:00**, so a weekday
  run means an early train up and a mid-afternoon train back. A weekend day removes the constraint
  entirely and is the better choice if the Florence week allows it.
- **Don't overstuff it.** One class *or* one long walk, one big lunch, one museum. The old two-night
  version had room for the checklist; one day does not.
- **Evening-only sights don't work.** Ustica (Fri–Sun 17:00–20:00) is the clearest casualty.
- **Sept weather swings** — layers and a rain jacket. The porticoes save you.
- **Bags.** Day trip means day bags. Everything stays at the Florence apartment.
