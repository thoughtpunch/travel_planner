## Base area — Garbatella / Ostiense

The "belt over center" call: bigger, quieter, cheaper one stop out but still on the line. Garbatella/Ostiense over the centro storico.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Vibe** | 1920s garden-city working-class neighborhood, generational Roman families, no tourists |
| **Architecture** | Curving Art Nouveau staircases, "lotti" with internal courtyards, very photogenic |
| **Transit** | Metro B "Garbatella" → Colosseum ~10 min, Termini ~15 min; Ostiense rail station on the doorstep |

## What's in Rome itself

### Big ancient sites
- **Colosseum + Roman Forum + Palatine** combo (book the timed combo ahead; 8:30am entry). Add the arena-floor or underground tour for the boys.
- **Castel Sant'Angelo** — Hadrian's tomb → papal fortress; ramps, ramparts, the secret papal escape passage, dungeons, best rooftop view in Rome.
- **Vatican Museums + Sistine + St Peter's** — book 8am early entry, avoid Wednesdays (papal audience). Don't miss the frescoed **Gallery of Maps / Sala delle Carte Geografiche** (very Voynich, Kei's thing).
- **San Clemente's buried city** — descend three stacked centuries under one church (12th-c basilica over 4th-c church over a 2nd-c house + a dark Mithras temple and an underground stream). Beats the standard catacombs.
- **Catacombs + Appian Way** — San Callisto or Domitilla underground, then bikes or a walk on the ancient road above.
- **Baths of Caracalla** — vast, roofless imperial bath ruins; open sky, towering brick, easy for the boys to run and imagine.
- **Capuchin Crypt** — five chapels decorated entirely in friars' bones. Macabre and beautiful; gauge a sensitive 9-year-old (Keir).

### For the grown-ups (baroque + weird-medieval)
- **Borghese Gallery** — Bernini's living marble (the Daphne) and Caravaggios in a garden villa. Timed entry, book well ahead.
- **Galleria Doria Pamphilj** — a private palace still lived in, Velázquez + Caravaggio, free audio guide by the prince himself. Calm and uncrowded.
- **Quartiere Coppedè** — a hidden pocket designed 1913–27 as one deranged fantasy: Art Nouveau meets Gothic meets Assyrian, Fountain of the Frogs, the Fairy House. Storybook-Escher weirdness, no tickets.
- **Museo delle Anime del Purgatorio** — one-room church museum of scorched "evidence" left by souls in purgatory. Free, eerie.

### For the boys
- **Gladiator school on the Via Appia** (Gruppo Storico Romano) — tunic, wooden sword, real moves, mini-tournament + certificate. The 9-year-old clears it (age 6+).
- **Centrale Montemartini** — Rome's first power station, now marble gods among diesel engines and turbines. Machine-meets-myth; 10 min from the Ostiense base (Jude's engineering brain).
- **Torre Argentina cat sanctuary** — ~150 rescued cats among the ruins where Caesar was stabbed (Keir).
- **Bocca della Verità** — the giant marble face that "bites off a liar's hand." Quick, cheap, funny dare, near the base.
- **The Aventine Keyhole** — peep through a locked green door and a hedge tunnel frames St Peter's dome perfectly; three sovereign states in one glance. Free.
- **Pyramid of Cestius & the Non-Catholic Cemetery** — a real Egyptian-style pyramid in Ostiense + the overgrown cemetery where Keats and Shelley sleep among a cat colony (Rhys). Steps from the base.
- Gelato crawl (Fatamorgana, Giolitti, Otaleg, La Romana).

The parents' bucket list, and genuinely fun for kids. Easiest with a **rental car for a single day** (the trip is otherwise carless — this is the one exception you'd make in Rome, and it's optional).

| Destination | Transit | Time | What |
|---|---|---|---|

## Daily rhythm (work-base week)

Dan works **evenings, ~4–11pm Italy time**, so mornings and early afternoons are the family's.

```
08:00  Breakfast at the apartment or a local Garbatella cafe
09:00  Out for the morning sight (Colosseum, Vatican, San Clemente, etc.)
13:00  Lunch out or pizza al taglio
14:30  Slow early afternoon — gelato, a lighter sight, back toward the base
16:00  Dan settles in for calls (4–11pm); family does a nearby low-key thing
19:30  Family dinner (out or in); Dan joins between/after calls when he can
22:00  Kids wind down; Dan finishes up
```

Because it's a full week in one place, no single day has to be big — spread the marquee sites across the seven days and leave room to just live in the neighborhood.

## What's hard about Rome

- **Cobblestones** are real on bad shoes; bring good walking footwear.
- **Late September is still warm** — apartment AC matters.
- **Crowds** at major sights — early/timed entry tickets are survival, not optional.
- **Distances** between sights are bigger than they look — lean on Metro B from the base.
- **Work-week juggling** — protect the mornings for family and keep afternoons flexible around Dan's 4–11pm calls.
