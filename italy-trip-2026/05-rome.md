### New since most guidebooks: Metro C reaches the Colosseum

**Colosseo/Fori Imperiali on Line C opened 16 December 2025**, along with Porta Metronia — and **San Giovanni is the A↔C interchange**. That quietly rewrites where it's sensible to stay: from the San Giovanni / Appio-Latino belt the ancient core is a couple of stops on A, change to C, and you surface at the Colosseum in roughly ten minutes. No bus, no long walk. Advice written before 2026 still treats that whole quadrant as "a bit out." It isn't, and the flats there are still priced as though it is.

### The neighborhood shortlist (kept for reference — and for Venice/Dolomites logic)

The rule, in one line: **anywhere within a 10-minute walk of a Metro A, B or C stop, inside the ring, that isn't a bar street.** Ranked by how much 2-bedroom-sleeps-6 inventory actually exists, not by guidebook charm.

| Area | Metro | 2BR/6 stock | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **San Giovanni / Re di Roma** | A (San Giovanni = A↔C) | **Lots** | Start here. Built for actual families. Colosseum ~10 min via Line C; Appian Way on the doorstep. Best value in the city |
| **Testaccio** (north of Monte Testaccio) | B · Piramide | Thin | Best fit, least stock. Walkable to five things on this page; evening scene downstairs. Grab it if it appears |
| **Ostiense / Garbatella** | B · Garbatella, San Paolo | **Good** | Cheap, quiet, reliable; Centrale Montemartini right there. A good answer, not a compromise |
| **Prati** (Cola di Rienzo/Lepanto) | A · Lepanto, Ottaviano | **Good** | Big Umbertine flats, calm, safe. Wrong end for Ostiense and the Appian Way |
| **Appio-Latino** | A · a few stops out | **Lots** | Same idea as San Giovanni, cheaper and further — **where we ended up** |
| Aventino | B · Circo Massimo | Very thin | Lovely, sleepy, and a hill. Walk up for the Keyhole; don't base here |
| Monteverde | none — tram 8 + buses | Good | Fails the one hard rule. A daily tax on every outing for a carless family. Skip |
| Monti / Trastevere | A / B · walk to everything | Thin + dear | Avoid. Loud exactly when Dan is on calls and Keir is asleep |

**The one question that decides a 2BR:** "sleeps 6" usually means 4 in beds and 2 on a living-room sofa bed — which breaks this trip, because the living room *is* Dan's office 4–11pm. Ask the host directly: *"Do all six sleep in the two bedrooms, or is one bed in the living room?"* A 2BR that sleeps six properly beats a 3BR whose third bedroom is a windowless box. Then confirm the **wifi speed in writing**, and check the **floor and whether there's a lift** (*ascensore*) — many Roman buildings are walk-ups, and late September still hits the high 20s°C, so AC matters too.

**The metro closes at 23:30 Sun–Thu** (01:30 Fri/Sat), after which the **nMB night bus** covers the Line B corridor. Weekday evenings the family will be out in the centro without Dan, so the last train home is a real planning constraint.

## What's in Rome itself

### Big ancient sites
- **Colosseum + Roman Forum + Palatine** combo (book the timed combo ahead; 8:30am entry). Add the arena-floor or underground tour for the boys.
- **Castel Sant'Angelo** — Hadrian's tomb → papal fortress; ramps, ramparts, the secret papal escape passage, dungeons, best rooftop view in Rome.
- **Vatican Museums + Sistine + St Peter's** — book 8am early entry, avoid Wednesdays (papal audience). Don't miss the frescoed **Gallery of Maps / Sala delle Carte Geografiche** — 1580 frescoed maps of all Italy in a glowing corridor (very Voynich, Kei's thing).
- **San Clemente's buried city** — descend three stacked centuries under one church (12th-c basilica over 4th-c church over a 2nd-c house + a dark Mithras temple and an underground stream). Beats the standard catacombs.
- **Catacombs + Appian Way** — San Callisto or Domitilla underground, then bikes or a walk on the ancient road above. Local from the base.
- **Baths of Caracalla** — vast, roofless imperial bath ruins; open sky, towering brick, easy for the boys to run and imagine.
- **Capuchin Crypt** — five chapels decorated entirely in friars' bones. Macabre and beautiful; gauge a sensitive 9-year-old (Keir).
- **Evening centro walk — Pantheon, Trevi, Navona** — the greatest free show in Rome, gelato in hand (Giolitti near the Pantheon). **After dark, so it belongs to the arrival weekend.**

### For the grown-ups (baroque + weird-medieval)
- **Borghese Gallery** — Bernini's living marble (the Daphne) and Caravaggios in a garden villa. Timed entry, book well ahead; compact enough that teens last the visit.
- **Galleria Doria Pamphilj** — a private palace still lived in, Velázquez + Caravaggio, free audio guide by the prince himself. Calm and uncrowded.
- **Quartiere Coppedè** — a hidden pocket designed 1913–27 as one deranged fantasy: Art Nouveau meets Gothic meets Assyrian, Fountain of the Frogs, the Fairy House, a spider-hung arch. Storybook-Escher weirdness, no tickets. Trieste district, tram 3/19.
- **Museo delle Anime del Purgatorio** — one-room church museum of scorched "evidence" left by souls in purgatory. Free, eerie. Lungotevere Prati.
- **Aperitivo in Trastevere + Testaccio eating** — Cesare al Casaletto, Flavio al Velavevodetto, the Testaccio market. Rome's best eating is working-class and south of the center.

### For the boys
- **Gladiator school on the Via Appia** (Gruppo Storico Romano) — tunic, wooden sword, real moves, mini-tournament + certificate. The 9-year-old clears it (age 6+). Local to the base.
- **Centrale Montemartini** — Rome's first power station, now marble gods among diesel engines and turbines. Machine-meets-myth; Metro B Garbatella (Jude's engineering brain).
- **Torre Argentina cat sanctuary** — ~150 rescued cats among the ruins where Caesar was stabbed; new 2023 walkways let you stroll among the columns. Shelter Tue–Sun midday–4:30pm (Keir).
- **Bocca della Verità** — the giant marble face that "bites off a liar's hand." Quick, cheap, funny dare; go early for the morning line.
- **The Aventine Keyhole** — peep through a locked green door and a hedge tunnel frames St Peter's dome perfectly; three sovereign states in one glance. Free, pair it with the Aventine orange garden.
- **Pyramid of Cestius & the Non-Catholic Cemetery** — a real Egyptian-style pyramid in Ostiense + the overgrown cemetery where Keats and Shelley sleep among a cat colony (Rhys). Metro B Piramide.
- **Explora Children's Museum + Villa Borghese** — hands-on kids' museum, then a family rickshaw in the Borghese gardens. Good decompression for Keir after big sites.
- Gelato crawl (Fatamorgana, Giolitti, Otaleg, La Romana).

## What's hard about Rome

- **Only two free evenings**, both in the arrival weekend — everything after dark competes for Sep 26 and 27.
- **Cobblestones** are real on bad shoes; bring good walking footwear.
- **Late September is still warm** — apartment AC matters.
- **Crowds** at major sights — early/timed entry tickets are survival, not optional.
- **Distances** between sights are bigger than they look — lean on Metro A and the new Line C interchange at San Giovanni.
- **The 23:30 last metro** Sun–Thu, on the nights the family is out without Dan.
- **Work-week juggling** — protect the mornings for family and keep afternoons flexible around Dan's 4–11pm calls.
