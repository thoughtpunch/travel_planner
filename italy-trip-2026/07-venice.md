## Why this stop exists — MuMu

Seeing **Aunt Muriel ("MuMu"), who lives on the Lido**, is one of the reasons this whole trip exists. These 5 nights are built **around her**, not as a Venice sightseeing add-on. Plan flexible, unhurried days: a long lunch that runs into the afternoon, a slow walk at her pace, a quiet evening in. Everything below is optional scenery around the real point — time with family. If a plan slips because MuMu wants one more coffee, that's the plan working.

Because it's short and family-first, **this is not a work base** — unlike Florence and Rome, Dan doesn't set up a 7-night evening-work rhythm here.

## Why the Lido, not San Marco

Same "belt over center" logic as Oltrarno and Garbatella — stay one stop out, still on the line:

- **MuMu is here** — basing on the Lido means we're where family is, not across the water in the tourist crush
- **Real neighborhood** — the Lido is a residential island with year-round locals, schools, supermarkets, an actual barbershop
- **Venice is ~12 minutes by vaporetto** — go when you want, leave when you're done
- **Cheaper and quieter** than San Marco apartments — easier sleep, more room for six
- **A beach island** — flat, bikeable, with sand and space to run when the city's had enough
- **On the line** — the Lido vaporetto stop connects straight into Venice; carless, like the rest of the trip (except the Dolomites)

## Venice Top 10 (built around the visits)

Five nights, MuMu first. Fit the sights around the visits — family-first, adult/weird picks marked.

1. **Murano glassblowing** — watch a maestro turn molten glass into a horse in two minutes, then try it: a furnace class for the teens or a torch-bead bench for the younger ones.
2. **St Mark's Basilica + golden mosaics** — 8,000 m² of gold Byzantine ceiling. Book a timed slot; add the Pala d'Oro and the terrace.
3. **Doge's Palace + the Armoury** — gilded halls, the Bridge of Sighs, dungeons, and 2,000+ pieces of arms and armor — the boys' jackpot. "Secret Itineraries" tour adds the prisons.
4. **Grand Canal by vaporetto (Line 1)** — the cheapest grand tour in the world, riding the slow boat past the palazzi. A gondola once for the romance; the vaporetto for daily life.
5. **San Michele — the cemetery island** — Venice's walled island of the dead: cypress avenues, a white Renaissance church, and the graves of Stravinsky, Diaghilev and Ezra Pound. One vaporetto stop off Fondamente Nove; contemplative, pairs naturally with a slow day near MuMu.
6. **Burano + Torcello islands** — Burano's candy-colored fishing houses and quiet Torcello with its ancient mosaic cathedral. A relaxed all-day boat outing across the lagoon.
7. **Libreria Acqua Alta** — the flooded bookshop where books live in a gondola and a bathtub, with a staircase made of old encyclopedias. Free, weird, delightful.
8. **Rialto market + cicchetti crawl** — the morning fish-and-produce market, then a tasting walk or a hands-on cicchetti class in a local's home. How Venetians actually eat.
9. **Lido beach + bikes** — your home island: rentable bikes and room to run, the low-key counterweight to museum days — and where the family is.
10. **Peggy Guggenheim / Accademia** — modern art in Peggy's canal-side home (compact, teen-friendly) or the Venetian masters at the Accademia. The grown-ups' art fix.

## Offbeat picks the boys will actually talk about

- **Scala Contarini del Bovolo** — a hidden 15th-c external spiral staircase up a brick tower to a rooftop belvedere. Pure Escher, down an alley most tourists never find. Timed slots.
- **Squero di San Trovaso** — one of Venice's last working gondola boatyards; watch craftsmen build and repair from the bank across the narrow rio. Free.
- **The €2 traghetto crossing** — the locals' hack: a two-oared traghetto gondola ferries you across the Grand Canal for ~€2, and tradition says you stand the whole way.
- **Ca' Macana mask workshop** — the atelier that made the masks for Kubrick's *Eyes Wide Shut* runs hands-on courses to build and decorate your own Carnival mask. Age 6+.
- **Torcello's Devil's Bridge & Attila's throne** — a railless stone bridge said to have been built by the devil in one night, and a marble "throne" that (legend says) means you'll return.
- **Burano's crooked campanile & Lace Museum** — a visibly leaning bell tower plus Buranese women demonstrating needle-lace by hand.
- **The plague islands by boat** — the lazzaretti and infamous Poveglia out in the lagoon; private "ghost island" tours circle the ruins. Older kids + adults.

## Getting in and out

- **In (Oct 4):** Frecciarossa Bologna → Venezia S. Lucia (~1h30), then vaporetto out to the Lido. Book this fast leg early — it's dynamic-priced like a flight. Pre-arrange a water taxi or plan the vaporetto with luggage on arrival.

## What's hard about Venice / Lido

- **Early October can bring acqua alta** — a high tide that floods St Mark's; the city lays out raised walkways (*passerelle*). Check the official tide forecast, pack boots, prefer a floor-1+ apartment.
- **Vaporetto with luggage** on arrival — pre-arrange a water taxi or porter on the Lido side.
- **Grocery-shop on the Lido itself** (Coop, Conad) rather than dragging bags back from Venice.
- **Mornings beat afternoons** for the crush — St Mark's at 8am or after 5pm.
