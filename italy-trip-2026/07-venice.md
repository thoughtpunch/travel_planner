## Why this stop exists — MuMu

Seeing **Aunt Muriel ("MuMu"), who lives on the Lido**, is one of the reasons this whole trip exists. These 7 nights are built **around her**, not as a Venice sightseeing add-on. Plan flexible, unhurried days: a long lunch that runs into the afternoon, a slow walk at her pace, a quiet evening in. Everything below is optional scenery around the real point — time with family. If a plan slips because MuMu wants one more coffee, that's the plan working.

The stop grew from five nights to seven, and the two extra nights are what make the week work: they buy the slack for a Padua day-trip without spending a MuMu day, they push the lodging over the **7-night weekly-rate threshold** (Airbnb and Booking both discount 10–20% at seven nights, on top of shoulder-season pricing — Florence, Rome and Venice/Lido are the three legs that clear it), and they put **our anniversary inside the Venice week**.

Because the week runs Sat → Sat, **Mon Oct 5 through Fri Oct 9 are normal workdays** for Dan (4–11pm Italy time). It isn't the full settle-in work rhythm of Florence and Rome, but the evenings aren't free either — plan MuMu time and the big outings into mornings and afternoons.

## Why the Lido, not San Marco

Same "belt over center" logic as Oltrarno and the Appio-Tuscolano belt — stay one stop out, still on the line:

- **MuMu is here** — basing on the Lido means we're where family is, not across the water in the tourist crush
- **Real neighborhood** — the Lido is a residential island with year-round locals, schools, supermarkets, an actual barbershop
- **Venice is ~12–15 minutes by vaporetto** — go when you want, leave when you're done
- **Cheaper and quieter** than San Marco apartments — easier sleep, more room for six
- **A beach island** — flat, bikeable, with sand and space to run when the city's had enough
- **On the line** — the Lido vaporetto stop connects straight into Venice, and on departure day straight out to the airport

## Venice Top 10 (built around the visits)

Seven nights, MuMu first. Fit the sights around the visits — family-first, adult/weird picks marked.

1. **Murano glassblowing** — watch a maestro turn molten glass into a horse in two minutes, then try it: a furnace class for the teens (Wave) or a torch-bead bench for the younger ones (VivoVenetia, age 10+).
2. **St Mark's Basilica + golden mosaics** — 8,000 m² of gold Byzantine ceiling, sacred-geometry overload for the parents. Book a timed slot; add the Pala d'Oro and the terrace.
3. **Doge's Palace + the Armoury** — gilded halls, the Bridge of Sighs, dungeons, and 2,000+ pieces of arms and armor (Henry IV's suit, Lepanto trophies) — the boys' jackpot. "Secret Itineraries" tour adds the prisons.
4. **Burano + Torcello islands** — Burano's candy-colored fishing houses and quiet Torcello with its ancient mosaic cathedral. A relaxed all-day boat outing across the lagoon.
5. **Grand Canal by vaporetto (Line 1)** — the cheapest grand tour in the world, riding the slow boat past the palazzi. A gondola once for the romance; the vaporetto for daily life.
6. **Rialto market + cicchetti crawl** — the morning fish-and-produce market, then a hands-on cicchetti class in a local's home (Cesarine) or a tasting walk. How Venetians actually eat.
7. **Libreria Acqua Alta** — the flooded bookshop where books live in a gondola and a bathtub, with a staircase made of old encyclopedias. Free, weird, delightful.
8. **Peggy Guggenheim / Accademia** — modern art in Peggy's canal-side home (compact, teen-friendly) or the Venetian masters at the Accademia. The grown-ups' art fix.
9. **Lido beach + bikes** — your home island: rentable bikes and room to run, the low-key counterweight to museum days — and where the family is.
10. **San Michele — the cemetery island** — Venice's walled island of the dead: cypress avenues, a white Renaissance church, and the graves of Stravinsky, Diaghilev and Ezra Pound. One vaporetto stop (4.1/4.2) off Fondamente Nove; contemplative, pairs naturally with a slow day near MuMu.

## Deeper cuts

**Grown-ups love**
- **Scuola Grande di San Rocco** — Tintoretto's overwhelming cycle of canvases, "Venice's Sistine Chapel," and rarely crowded.
- **Cannaregio & the Jewish Ghetto** — the world's first ghetto, quiet canals, and the best cicchetti bars along Fondamenta della Misericordia. Real, lived-in Venice at aperitivo hour.
- **Homo Faber** — Venice's master-craft biennial on San Giorgio, the best craft show in Europe. **Check whether it overlaps the Oct 3–10 window.**

**Family wins**
- **Climb the Campanile + the Torre dell'Orologio** — the bell-tower lift for the big view, and the clock-tower tour up inside the blue-and-gold astronomical clock (mechanism + figures).
- **Natural History Museum** — dinosaurs and a giant whale skeleton in a Grand Canal palazzo. Easy rainy-day hit for the 9-year-old.
- **Get lost on purpose** — hand the kids a map and let them navigate the back-canal labyrinth to a gelato. Venice is the safest big city to wander.

## Offbeat picks the boys will actually talk about

- **Scala Contarini del Bovolo** — a hidden 15th-c external spiral staircase, 80 stone steps twisting counter-clockwise up a 26 m brick tower to a rooftop belvedere. Pure Escher, down an alley most tourists never find. Timed 30-min slots, max 20 people, under-12s free.
- **Squero di San Trovaso** — one of Venice's last working gondola boatyards, open since the 17th century; you can't go in, but watch the craftsmen build and repair from the bank across the narrow Rio San Trovaso. Free, Dorsoduro/Zattere.
- **The €2 traghetto crossing** — the locals' hack: a two-oared traghetto gondola ferries you across the Grand Canal for ~€2, and tradition says you stand the whole way. Seven crossing points, cash.
- **Ca' Macana mask workshop** — the Dorsoduro atelier that made the masks for Kubrick's *Eyes Wide Shut* runs hands-on courses to build and decorate your own Carnival mask in traditional papier-mâché. Age 6+, book ahead.
- **Torcello's Devil's Bridge & Attila's throne** — a railless stone bridge said to have been built by the devil in one night to win a bet, and a marble "throne" that (legend says) means you'll return.
- **Burano's crooked campanile & Lace Museum** — the visibly tilting *campanile storto*, plus Buranese women demonstrating needle-lace by hand on Galuppi Square.
- **The plague islands by boat** — the lazzaretti and infamous Poveglia out in the lagoon. You can't land on Poveglia, but private "ghost island" tours circle the ruins and land at Lazzaretto Vecchio. Older kids + adults.
- **Acqua alta & the raised walkways** — if the tide comes, the city's answer is wonderfully odd: instant elevated *passerelle* snapped together on trestles so life goes on above the water. Check the tide forecast and watch a city calmly walk on stilts.

## What's hard about Venice / Lido

- **Early October can bring acqua alta** — a high tide that floods St Mark's; the city lays out raised *passerelle*. Check the official tide forecast, pack boots, prefer a floor-1+ apartment, and keep a backup for the anniversary dinner.
- **Vaporetto with luggage** on arrival — pre-arrange a water taxi or porter on the Lido side. Departure day is easier: the Alilaguna runs Lido → airport direct.
- **Grocery-shop on the Lido itself** (Coop, Conad) rather than dragging bags back from Venice.
- **Mornings beat afternoons** for the crush — St Mark's at 8am or after 5pm.
- **Five of the seven nights are Dan's workdays** (Mon Oct 5 – Fri Oct 9) — the anniversary Friday has to be carved out of that block deliberately.
