Set the passenger ages at booking and it applies automatically. Italo has the same scheme; compare it on all three legs, it's often cheaper on the same route.

**Read the drop date carefully — it changes the last two days.** We drive **Kastelruth → the Malpensa area on Wed Oct 14** (~355 km / ~4h15, motorway throughout, with a lunch stop at **Sirmione on Lake Garda**), **sleep near the airport that night with the car**, and hand it back at **noon on Thu Oct 15** — four hours before the 16:20 flight. **Why a 7-seater and not a van.** Six people need six belts and Europe has no 6-seat class — the jump is 5 → 7. With six aboard you fold one third-row seat down for luggage, which works precisely because we're carry-on only.

### 🔴 The International Driving Permits — the one car item still outstanding, and the one that can't be fixed later

**Dan and Kei each need an IDP** — ~$20 at AAA, issued over the counter, bring a passport photo and your license. Italy requires it alongside a US license and **the Budget desk at Marco Polo can refuse the car without one** — which would strand a booked, paid, non-negotiable leg of the trip. **This is now the single most forgettable thing on this page and the only one that is unrecoverable once you've left the country.** Do it this week.

### The other vehicle: the Malpensa arrival transfer

You land **21:55 Fri Sep 11** with six people and bags. Book a **pre-booked 6-seat van transfer** (fixed ~€100–120) straight to the San Siro apartment. The Malpensa Express (~50 min, €13pp, runs past midnight) is the daylight option — at that hour, take the van. Book it now; late-night 6-seaters are a finite pool.

## What NOT to book ahead — on purpose

Beyond the must-books above, keep the trip loose. With four boys the day bends to moods and weather.

- **Restaurant reservations more than ~2 weeks out** — except the three milestone dinners. You won't know how the kids feel that night.
- **Daily museum tickets** past the timed entries listed. Day-of walk-up almost always works.
- **The Dolomites scenic drives** — Gardena and Sella passes, the Sellaronda, Passo Pordoi. Nothing to book; go when the weather's good.
- **Aperitivo / nightlife** — purely walk-up.
- **Workshops or tours you might bail on** — book in-country once it's actually happening.
- **The flat-fare regionale legs** — locking them early buys nothing and costs flexibility.
- **A third Dolomites activity.** Four nights is **only three full days: Oct 11, 12, 13.** Oct 10 is a travel afternoon (Alilaguna + car pickup + the drive up), Oct 14 is the drive to Malpensa. Oct 12 belongs to Grey. So **Oct 11 and Oct 13 are already spoken for** by Kronplatz and Jude's rocks/minerals day — which means **Alpe di Siusi has no slot left.** It's either cut, or squeezed into the Oct 10 arrival afternoon if the drive goes quickly. Don't book a fourth thing into three days.

## Two calendar blocks, not bookings

- **Mon Oct 12** — blocked for Grey's birthday, no work window.
- **Wed Oct 14 and Thu Oct 15** — both need **PTO**. The 14th is a ~4h30 drive to the Malpensa area, straight through Dan's 16:00–23:00 window; the 15th is car back at MXP 12:00, then the 16:20 flight home.
- **Tue Oct 13** is the only normal work evening in the Dolomites — keep that day's plan close to base.
