— The playbook

The money-saving levers

Ranked by dollars saved. None of these cost you anything you'd actually miss — they're the difference between the high case and the low.

Biggest lever

Weekly & monthly lodging rates

Book Florence and Rome (7 nights each) to trigger the weekly discount — 10–20% off nightly — and whole-place with a kitchen everywhere. If any base can stretch toward 28 nights, the monthly rate is bigger still.

saves $1,000–1,800

Cook more

4–5 dinners in, not out

Every home dinner for 6 beats a trattoria by ~$100. Shop Lidl / Eurospin / Conad and the markets — Mercato Centrale, Testaccio, the Bologna Quadrilatero, Rialto — not tourist alimentari.

saves $1,200–1,800

Free by law

Under-18s free at state sites

Grey (12), Keir (9) and Rhys (until Sep 18) enter the Colosseum, Uffizi, Doge's Palace, Accademia and every Italian state site free. Pay only for private experiences — classes, boats, cable cars. Rhys pays adult after his birthday.

saves $500–800

Smarter trains

Regionale + family discounts

Use cheap Regionale for the short hops and day trips, book Frecce early-bird for the long legs, and stack Trenitalia's Bimbi Gratis (under-15s free with adults — covers Grey and Keir). See Trains.

saves $400–700

Buy passes, not singles

Transit passes over one-offs

A 7-day ACTV vaporetto pass on the Lido crushes single fares; a Cinque Terre Card bundles the coast trains + trails; Genoa and the coast towns have day tickets. Buy the pass the moment you'll ride more than twice.

saves $200–400

The one splurge, kept tight

Self-cater the Dolomites

The chalet leg is meant to cost — but a kitchen + a supermarket run in Ortisei keeps the priciest region from running away. Save the blowout for Grey's birthday dinner, groceries the rest.

saves $300–500

Pull the top four and you're at the low case Weekly rates + cook 4–5 nights + under-18-free + smart trains is the difference between the $25K high and the ~$16–18K low — with nothing cut from the trip. Layer on the card/FX game (dining multipliers, a Wise EUR balance for groceries) for a few hundred more back.
