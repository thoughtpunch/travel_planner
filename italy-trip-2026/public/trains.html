— The one thing to understand first

Two kinds of train, two kinds of ticket

Get this and the rest is easy. Italy runs two totally different products, and the buying rules are different for each.

Le Frecce / Italo — high-speed

The fast, reserved, seat-assigned trains (Frecciarossa is Trenitalia; Italo is the private competitor on the same routes). Price is dynamic — like flights. A Florence→Rome seat is ~€19 booked a month out and ~€55 the day of. Book these early. Every seat is a specific seat; miss the train and the cheap "Super Economy" fare is gone. We use these for Florence→Rome, Rome→Bologna, Bologna→Venice.

Regionale — the local network

Flat price, never sells out, no reservation. Buy it five minutes before or five weeks before — same fare. No assigned seat. This is how you ride Milan→Como, the Cinque Terre coast line, and short hops. One rule: on paper tickets you must validate/self-check-in before boarding (the app ticket auto-activates). Slower, but for our short legs the time difference is tiny and the savings real.

The family discount that pays for itself — "Offerta Famiglia" On Le Frecce and Intercity, a group of 2–5 that includes at least one child under 15 travels as a family: the under-15s ride FREE and the adults get 20% off. Grey (11, turns 12 on Oct 12) and Keir (9) are both under 15. Because a family booking maxes at 5 people, split our six into two bookings, each carrying one of the little ones — e.g. {Dan · Kei · Grey} and {Rhys · Jude · Keir} — so both bookings unlock the discount. Rhys (18) and Jude (16) pay adult fares. Italo has the same idea ("Italo Famiglia"). Set the passenger ages when booking and it applies automatically.

— The airport ends

Getting in and out

Fri Sep 11 · arrival

Malpensa → Milan, 9:55pm

You land late and tired with six people and bags. Two clean options: the Malpensa Express train to Milano Centrale/Cadorna (~50 min, €13pp, runs past midnight), or — easier at that hour — a pre-booked 6-seat van transfer (fixed ~€100–120) straight to the apartment. Book the van; save the train for daylight.

Oct 9–14 · the one car

The Dolomites rental

Collect in Bolzano after the train, return there before the Milan leg. A 6-seater/SUV or small van for 5 days; reserve early (small-city depots run out). This is the only leg you drive — Val Gardena, the passes, Alpe di Siusi. Watch for ZTL limited-traffic zones and buy an autostrada Telepass or pay tolls as you go.

— Local transit + the money-savers

Passes that do the heavy lifting

Beyond the intercity legs, three of our stops run on their own local networks. Buy the right pass and a whole day of hopping is one flat ticket.

Cinque Terre · Sep 16–18

Cinque Terre Card (Treno)

Unlimited local trains Levanto↔La Spezia (all five villages), trail access, buses and wifi on one card — 1-day ~€19.50 adult / discounted for kids, or a 2-day. The village-hopping train comes every ~20 min. Buy at any station or the park site.

parconazionale5terre.it
Lake Como · Sep 13–16

Navigazione Lago di Como

The lake is the highway. A central-lake day pass (Como–Bellagio–Varenna–Menaggio) gives free ferry hopping for the Bellagio/Varenna day; single hops otherwise. Pair with the Como–Brunate funicular (separate ~€6 return).

navigazionelaghi.it
Venice · Oct 4–9

Vaporetto travel card

ACTV runs the water buses. From the Lido base you'll ride daily — a multi-day ACTV pass (2/3/7-day) beats single €9.50 tickets fast, and covers Murano/Burano/Torcello. Kids under 6 free.

veneziaunica.it

Four painless ways to hold the number down

01 · Book the 3 Frecce early

Florence→Rome, Rome→Bologna, Bologna→Venice are dynamic-priced. "Super Economy" fares open ~4 months out and sell in tiers — buying early can halve them. Set the ages so Famiglia applies (kids free, adults −20%).

02 · Regionale for the short hops

Milan→Como and the Cinque Terre coast are flat-fare Regionale — no reason to pay high-speed prices. Same for a Bologna→Venice Regionale Veloce if the Freccia fare is high that day.

03 · One app, one wallet

Buy in the Trenitalia app (tickets auto-validate, no paper to stamp). Compare Italo on the three high-speed legs — it's often cheaper on the same route. Trainline works too but adds a small fee.

04 · Travel off-peak, mid-day

Dan works evenings, so we move late-morning/early-afternoon anyway — which dodges commuter peaks and the priciest high-speed departures. Weekend relocations are usually cheaper than Friday-evening ones.

Book in this order

1
The three Frecciarossa legs (Florence→Rome, Rome→Bologna, Bologna→Venice) the day fares open — with passenger ages set so Famiglia applies.
Biggest single saving. ~4 months ahead.
2
The Dolomites rental car (Bolzano pickup/return, 6-seater, Oct 9–14) and the Malpensa arrival van.
Small-city depots + late-night transfers sell out — lock early.
3
Regionale legs (Milan→Como, Como→Cinque Terre, Cinque Terre→Florence, Venice→Bolzano) — buy same-week, no rush, flat fares.
Plus the Cinque Terre Card, Como ferry pass, and a Venice vaporetto card on arrival.
