— The one thing to understand first

Two kinds of train, two kinds of ticket

Get this and the rest is easy. Italy runs two totally different products, and the buying rules are different for each.

Le Frecce / Italo — high-speed

The fast, reserved, seat-assigned trains (Frecciarossa is Trenitalia; Italo is the private competitor on the same routes). Price is dynamic — like flights. A Florence→Rome seat is ~€19 booked a month out and ~€55 the day of. Book these early. Every seat is a specific seat; miss the train and the cheap "Super Economy" fare is gone. We use these for exactly three legs: the opener Milan→Turin, then Florence→Rome and Rome→Venice. There is no train back to Malpensa — the rental car does that run.

Regionale — the local network

Flat price, never sells out, no reservation. Buy it five minutes before or five weeks before — same fare. No assigned seat. This is how you ride Genoa→Chiavari, the Cinque Terre Express coast line, and short hops. One rule: on paper tickets you must validate/self-check-in before boarding (the app ticket auto-activates). Slower, but for our short legs the time difference is tiny and the savings real.

The family discount that pays for itself — "Offerta Famiglia" On Le Frecce and Intercity, a group of 2–5 that includes at least one child under 15 travels as a family: the under-15s ride FREE and the adults get 20% off. Grey (11, turns 12 on Oct 12) and Keir (9) are both under 15. Because a family booking maxes at 5 people, split our six into two bookings, each carrying one of the little ones — e.g. {Dan · Kei · Grey} and {Rhys · Jude · Keir} — so both bookings unlock the discount. Rhys (18) and Jude (16) pay adult fares. Italo has the same idea ("Italo Famiglia"). Set the passenger ages when booking and it applies automatically.

You land late and tired with six people and bags. Two clean options: the Malpensa Express train to Milano Centrale/Cadorna (~50 min, €13pp, runs past midnight), or — easier at that hour — a pre-booked 6-seat van transfer (fixed ~€100–120) straight to the apartment. Book the van; save the train for daylight.

The Dolomites rental

Booked — Budget, ref 02391839US2. Peugeot 5008 or similar, 7 seats, automatic, unlimited mileage, CDW + theft protection, free cancellation. Budget: +1 866-671-7282.

Pick up Sat Oct 10, 12:00, Venice Marco Polo (VCE, Multipiano P1, terzo piano) — the Alilaguna boat runs straight from the Lido to the airport, so there's no train and no hauling six people's bags to S. Lucia. Drop Thu Oct 15, 12:00, Malpensa Terminal 1 — one-way. That single rental replaces both old rail legs.

Note the drop is the 15th, not the 14th: we drive out on Wed Oct 14, sleep with the car at the Osteria della Pista in Casorate Sempione (9 min from the terminal), and hand it back at noon on flight day. That leaves Thursday morning unhurried — repack at your own pace rather than at 6am — and still puts us at Terminal 1 four hours before the 16:20 departure.

Book a 7-seater, not a van. Six people need six belts and Europe has no 6-seat class — the jump is 5 → 7 (Peugeot 5008, Škoda Kodiaq, VW Touran, SEAT Tarraco). With six aboard you fold one third-row seat for the bags. Bolzano was rejected: its whole depot showed two 7+ seat offers, both 9-seat vans at ~€1,150 — a stockout risk we don't need.

Watch for ZTL limited-traffic zones in the village centres (and make sure the chalet has off-street parking), budget ~€35–45 of autostrada tolls on the Malpensa run, and note that a US licence legally needs an International Driving Permit in Italy — ~$20 at AAA, get one each for Dan and Kei.

— Local transit + the money-savers

Passes that do the heavy lifting

Beyond the intercity legs, two of our stops run on their own local networks. Buy the right pass and a whole day of hopping is one flat ticket.

Ligurian Coast · Sep 15–19

Cinque Terre Card (Treno)

From the Chiavari base, ride the regionale to Levanto/Monterosso, then the card gives unlimited local trains Levanto↔La Spezia (all five villages), trail access, buses and wifi — 1-day ~€19.50 adult / discounted for kids, or a 2-day. The Cinque Terre Express comes every ~20 min. Buy at any station or the park site.

parconazionale5terre.it
Venice · Oct 3–10

Vaporetto travel card

ACTV runs the water buses. From the Lido base you'll ride daily — a multi-day ACTV pass (2/3/7-day) beats single €9.50 tickets fast, and covers Murano/Burano/Torcello. Kids under 6 free.

veneziaunica.it

Four painless ways to hold the number down

01 · Book the Frecce early

Milan→Turin, Florence→Rome and Rome→Venice are dynamic-priced (there is no rail leg back to Malpensa — the rental car covers the run out of the Dolomites). "Super Economy" fares open ~4 months out and sell in tiers, so the cheap tiers are already gone — with departure inside three weeks these should be bought NOW, before they climb again. Booking the day before roughly triples a Florence→Rome seat. Set the ages so Famiglia applies (kids free, adults −20%).

02 · Regionale for the short hops

Genoa→Chiavari and the Cinque Terre Express coast line are flat-fare Regionale — no reason to pay high-speed prices. Same for the Chiavari→Florence Regionale Veloce and the Bologna food day-trip if the Freccia fare is high that day.

03 · One app, one wallet

Buy in the Trenitalia app (tickets auto-validate, no paper to stamp). Compare Italo on the three high-speed legs — it's often cheaper on the same route. Trainline works too but adds a small fee.

04 · Travel off-peak, mid-day

Dan works evenings, so we move late-morning/early-afternoon anyway — which dodges commuter peaks and the priciest high-speed departures. Weekend relocations are usually cheaper than Friday-evening ones.
