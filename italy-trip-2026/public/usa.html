Two separate tickets, one nested inside the other. The cheap Avianca SJO⇄IAD fare runs Sep 9 → Nov 17; the Icelandair BWI⇄Milan ticket sits inside it, Sep 10 → Oct 15. The consequence people get wrong: there is no US time before Italy — the outbound is a same-day sprint from Dulles to Baltimore. Every one of the 32 US nights lands on the back end, Oct 15 through Nov 16, and every one of them is with family: Katelin's in King George, Carrie's in Bostic, and Amy's — Kei's sister — four miles from Dulles for the gap days at either end. No hotels anywhere in the US block.

— How the two tickets interlock

One ticket inside another

The Avianca booking is the outer bread: it opens Sep 9 in San José and doesn't close until Nov 17. The Icelandair booking is the filling, and it only touches US soil at BWI — an airport the Avianca ticket never visits. That mismatch, IAD on one ticket and BWI on the other, is the whole logistical puzzle.

The 01:30 departure is a Nov 16 evening, not a Nov 17 day The homebound Avianca flight leaves Dulles at 01:30 on Tuesday Nov 17 — which means checking in on the evening of Monday Nov 16. Book the last night's lodging (or the last drive) against Nov 16. This is the single easiest date on the whole trip to misread by 24 hours.
Both fares are carry-on only — which is what makes the transfer survivable Avianca Light: 1 personal item + 1 carry-on (10 kg), 0 checked. Icelandair Economy Light: 1 carry-on, 0 checked. Because nothing is checked, nothing can be mis-tagged between the two separate tickets and there's no bag to reclaim at IAD before the run to BWI. The carry-on-only discipline you already planned for Italy is doing double duty here.
— The one real seam

Sep 10 — Dulles to Baltimore, on your own

You land at IAD at 04:20 and Icelandair leaves BWI at 23:20. That's a 19-hour window to cover roughly an hour of road. The cushion is enormous — this is a comfortable day, not a scramble. But two things are genuinely true and worth naming.

Risk · separate tickets

Icelandair owes you nothing

These are two unconnected bookings. If Avianca runs late, Icelandair has no obligation to rebook you and no through-fare protection applies. The mitigation is already baked in: 19 hours of slack absorbs essentially any delay short of a full-day cancellation. Just don't let the buffer tempt you into booking something rigid mid-day.

Verdict: low risk, because the cushion is huge · don't shrink it

Immigration · where it happens

You clear the US at Dulles

Arriving from San José, you clear immigration and customs at IAD in the small hours, then walk out into the US as a domestic traveler. The BWI departure that night is an ordinary check-in. Six passports at 04:30 is slow but the queues are thin at that hour.

All six · expect the process, not a rush

The road · ~1 hour

IAD → BWI, and the options

Roughly 50 miles around the north side of DC, typically an hour outside rush hour — but the DC Beltway can double that on a weekday afternoon. Leave a generous margin and aim to be at BWI by early evening. A single 6-seat van rideshare, a one-day rental dropped at BWI, or a pre-booked shuttle all work; with six people and a 04:30 arrival, pre-booking beats hailing.

Six people + six carry-ons · book the vehicle ahead, not on arrival

Best use of the day

Udvar-Hazy is 10 minutes away

The Smithsonian's Steven F. Udvar-Hazy Center sits minutes from IAD in Chantilly — the big hangar annex with Space Shuttle Discovery, an SR-71 Blackbird and a Concorde. Admission is free, open 10:00–17:30 daily, parking $15 (free after 4pm). It is almost absurdly well-placed for a forced layover: kill five hours somewhere genuinely great, then drive to BWI.

All four boys · free · you'll be jet-lagged, so go easy

Price that last one honestly — a single 32-day round-trip rental sometimes beats two shorter one-ways once drop fees land.
— Wheels

The rental car — roughly half the block

The plan is a car for the King George half: it meets you at the airport, covers rural Virginia where nothing is walkable, and carries you south to North Carolina. At Carrie's you're on household vehicles. The open question is whether it goes back at that point or stays for the month — because you still have to get from Bostic to Amy's on Nov 12.

Settled · leg 1

BWI, Oct 15 → NC, Oct 29/30

Pick up on arrival at BWI the night of Oct 15 (confirm the counter is staffed past 22:15 — several BWI desks close around midnight, and a 22:15 landing plus immigration-free domestic arrival should clear it, but verify before you rely on it). Hold it through the King George stay and the drive south, then drop in North Carolina. That's ~15 days — the "half" you budgeted.

6 passengers + 6 carry-ons · you need a minivan or large SUV, book early

Watch this · pricing trap

The one-way drop fee

Dropping a Maryland pickup in western North Carolina is a one-way rental and the drop fee can be brutal — sometimes more than the rental itself. Price all three before booking: drop at Charlotte (CLT), drop at Asheville (AVL), or keep a round-trip and return it north. Occasionally a 32-day round-trip beats a 15-day one-way outright.

Price CLT vs AVL vs round-trip · the gap is often hundreds

Open · the last decision

Drop it in NC, or hold it the whole month?

If the car goes back on Oct 30 you're carless in rural NC and still need to reach Dulles on Nov 12. So price the two shapes against each other: one-way BWI→NC for 15 days plus a second arrangement north, versus one round-trip vehicle for all 32 days dropped at IAD. The second is more car-days but no drop fee, no second booking, and no scramble — it often wins outright.

Decide this before booking anything — it defines the whole US block

Either way · the last leg

Nov 16 is now trivial

Whatever you decide above, the final morning is easy: Amy's is four miles from the terminal. Drop the rental at IAD during the day on Nov 16, spend the evening at Amy's, and take a ten-minute ride to check in for the 01:30 departure. No pre-dawn drive, no airport hotel, no long-haul day before a long-haul flight.

Return the car to IAD, not BWI — cheaper and closer
