— The shortlist

Rome Top 10

Ranked for a family that includes a 9-year-old and two parents who love the weird-medieval and the baroque. EU under-18s are free at all state sites.

Dan gets exactly two evenings in Rome — and they're both at the start The week runs Sat Sep 26 → Sat Oct 3, and the 4–11pm calls eat Monday to Friday. That leaves Saturday the 26th and Sunday the 27th as the only nights Dan is free, and you land on the 26th at 08:35. So the things that are explicitly after dark — the Pantheon–Trevi–Navona walk at #5, Rhys's aperitivo, a long dinner out — have to be front-loaded into the arrival weekend or Dan simply misses them. Everything else is a morning. Plan the week backwards from those two nights.

— Weird, wacky & unforgettable

Offbeat & hidden gems

The strange, memorable, off-the-tourist-track finds the boys will actually talk about — vetted, real, and — five of the eight below — walkable from the Aventino/Testaccio base.

Keir · peek-through illusion

The Aventine Keyhole

Put your eye to a locked green door at the Knights of Malta priory and a hedge tunnel frames St Peter's dome perfectly — three sovereign states in one glance. A tiny, free, only-in-Rome trick that hits Keir's love of secret peepholes and the parents' Escher-illusion streak.

All ages · free, no ticket · short line, pair with the Aventine orange garden

turismoroma.it
Jude & parents · macabre

Museo delle Anime del Purgatorio

A one-room museum in a neo-Gothic church on the Tiber displaying "evidence" of the dead — scorched handprints and burn marks souls supposedly seared onto prayer books and clothing to beg for release from purgatory. Free, eerie, and pure Voynich for the parents; gore-and-curiosities catnip for Jude.

Older kids + adults · free · Lungotevere Prati, open morning & late afternoon

turismoroma.it
Jude · engineering + antiquity

Centrale Montemartini

Rome's first power station, now a museum where white marble gods and emperors stand among the black diesel engines, steam turbines and boilers left exactly in place. The machine-meets-myth contrast is unforgettable and speaks straight to Jude's engineering brain — and it's a short walk down through Ostiense from the Testaccio base.

All ages · ticketed, rarely crowded · Metro B Garbatella

centralemontemartini.org
Keir · animals + history

Torre Argentina cat sanctuary

Down in the sunken square where Julius Caesar was actually stabbed, ~150 rescued cats now lounge among the temple ruins. New 2023 walkways let you stroll among the columns, and the volunteer shelter lets you meet the residents. Caesar's murder scene plus a colony of cats is peak Keir.

All ages · free (donations) · shelter Tue–Sun midday–4:30pm

gattidiroma.net
Grey & Keir · legend + a dare

Bocca della Verità

The giant ancient marble face in the portico of Santa Maria in Cosmedin — legend says it bites off a liar's hand. Everyone takes the dare (and the photo, straight out of Roman Holiday). A quick, cheap, funny stop that the younger boys will make a game of, right by the base.

All ages · small donation · get there early, morning line

turismoroma.it
Parents · fairytale-Escher block

Quartiere Coppedè

A hidden pocket of Rome designed 1913–27 as one deranged fantasy: Art Nouveau meets Gothic meets Assyrian, a Fountain of the Frogs, a Fairy House, a spider-hung arch. No tickets, no crowds, one dreamlike square — exactly the storybook-Escher weirdness the parents collect.

All ages · free, open street · Trieste district, tram 3/19

turismoroma.it
All · time-machine underground

San Clemente's buried city

Descend through three stacked centuries under one church: a 12th-century basilica over a 4th-century church over a 2nd-century house — with a dark pagan Mithras temple and an underground stream running at the bottom. A literal dig through time that beats any of the standard catacombs on the page.

All ages · ~€10 underground ticket · near the Colosseum

basilicasanclemente.com
Rhys & parents · pyramid + poets + cats

Pyramid of Cestius & the Non-Catholic Cemetery

A real 2,000-year-old Egyptian-style pyramid tomb rises out of Ostiense, and behind it sleeps a romantic overgrown cemetery where Keats and Shelley are buried and a cat colony naps on the tombstones. Quiet, literary and a little gothic — a good moody wander for Rhys, and steps from our base.

All ages · cemetery donation entry · Metro B Piramide

cemeteryrome.it

The reasoning that got us here is kept below — useful for Venice and the Dolomites, which are still open.

Whole place, sleeps 6, 2+ bedrooms, on a metro line, Sep 26 – Oct 3. That's the whole brief — and it's deliberately loose, because at this budget and this size the binding constraint is inventory, not taste. Filter to 3 bedrooms and Rome nearly empties out. Filter to 2 and the city opens up.

The rule, in one line Anywhere within a 10-minute walk of a Metro A, B or C stop, inside the ring, that isn't a bar street. That's it. Below is that shortlist, ranked by how much 2-bedroom-sleeps-6 inventory actually exists — not by which neighbourhood reads best in a guidebook.
New since most guidebooks: Metro C reaches the Colosseum Colosseo/Fori Imperiali on Line C opened 16 December 2025, along with Porta Metronia — and San Giovanni is the A↔C interchange. This quietly rewrites where it's sensible to stay. From the San Giovanni / Appio-Latino belt the ancient core is now a couple of stops on A to San Giovanni, change to C, and you surface at the Colosseum — roughly ten minutes, no bus, no long walk. Advice written before 2026 still treats that whole quadrant as "a bit out." It isn't any more, and the flats there are still priced as though it is.
Testaccio is still the nicest fit — it's just small If a 2-bedroom sleeping six comes up in the box bounded by Piazza Testaccio → Via Marmorata → Piramide → the Aventine edge, take it. Five things on this page are walkable from there, the Colosseum is one stop, and the evening scene is downstairs — which matters more than usual here, because Dan is on calls till 11pm Mon–Fri and Kei will be taking the boys out for pizza without him. Being back in five minutes beats a 23:30 last train. Stay north of Monte Testaccio, where the clubs are, and prefer the flat streets off Via Marmorata to the Aventine hill.

But Testaccio is a physically tiny neighbourhood. If it's empty, don't force it — San Giovanni is the volume answer and it's genuinely good.

At 2 bedrooms, one question decides everything: where does the 6th person sleep? "Sleeps 6" in a 2-bedroom usually means 4 in beds and 2 on a living-room sofa bed. That configuration breaks this trip — because the living room is Dan's office from 4pm to 11pm every weekday, and he can't work in a room where two boys are trying to sleep. It's the single thing to check before anything else.

What you need is a 2BR where all six sleep inside the two bedrooms — realistically a parents' room plus a second room with two bunks, or a double plus twins. Then the living room stays free and the whole week works. Ask the host directly: "Do all six sleep in the two bedrooms, or is one bed in the living room?" A 2BR that sleeps 6 properly beats a 3BR where the third "bedroom" is a windowless box.

Then the usual two: confirm the wifi speed in writing, and check the floor and whether there's a lift (ascensore) — many Roman buildings are walk-ups, and late September still hits the high 20s°C, so AC matters too.
The metro closes at 23:30 Sun–Thu Weekday evenings the family will be out in the centro without Dan. Last metro is 23:30 Sunday through Thursday (01:30 Fri/Sat), after which the nMB night bus covers the Line B corridor — which is another quiet argument for basing on Line B rather than somewhere that needs a change.
