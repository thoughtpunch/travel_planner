— The shortlist

Florence Top 10

Ranked for a family with a 9-year-old, teenage boys and two parents who love the weird and the hands-on. Family rule: one major Renaissance gallery, not five. EU under-18s are free at all state museums (Uffizi, Accademia, Bargello).

  1. 1
    Duomo complex + climb the dome
    book timed · duomo.firenze.it

    Climb Brunelleschi's dome (463 steps, up between the two shells past the frescoes) and/or Giotto's campanile for the rival view. The single best morning in Florence — book the timed dome slot well ahead.

    All ages · climb 6+ · duomo.firenze.it
  2. 2
    Stibbert Museum — armor & samurai
    off the tourist track · a villa museum

    An eccentric collector's house crammed with European armor and a full hall of Japanese samurai armor and swords. The boys' and Kei's favorite, and blissfully uncrowded — worth the short ride out.

    All ages · museostibbert.it
  3. 3
    Museo Galileo
    by the Uffizi · astrolabes & automata

    Astrolabes, armillary spheres, early automata, Galileo's own telescopes — and, preserved in a glass egg, his actual finger. Sacred-geometry, curiosity-cabinet energy: peak Voynich for the parents.

    All ages · museogalileo.it
  4. 4
    HZERO — miniature railway world
    Palazzo Bombicci · huge model layout

    An enormous, obsessively detailed model railway and miniature city — trains, tiny towns, day-to-night lighting. A pure delight for Keir and Grey, and honestly mesmerizing for everyone.

    All ages · hzero.com
  5. 5
    Palazzo Vecchio — secret passages + tower
    the "Monster" / hidden-passages tour + climb

    Book the secret-passages / Monster tour to slip through hidden staircases behind the state rooms, then climb the Arnolfo tower over the piazza. Storytelling adventure the kids actually engage with.

    All ages · tour 4+ · museicivicifiorentini
  6. 6
    A hands-on artisan workshop
    Oltrarno · Florence's signature day

    Make something with your hands: leather at the Scuola del Cuoio, paper-marbling and bookbinding, mosaic, or metal-embossing. The most Florentine thing you can do and everyone leaves with an object.

    Family · book ahead · scuoladelcuoio.com
  7. 7
    ONE big-hitter: Uffizi OR Accademia
    pick one · book timed entry

    The family rule in action. Botticelli's Birth of Venus and Primavera at the Uffizi, or Michelangelo's David at the Accademia — choose one, go early, keep it tight. Under-18s free.

    All ages · uffizi.it · b-ticket.com
  8. 8
    La Specola natural-history museum
    recently reopened · Oltrarno

    Rooms of taxidermy and, most famously, the 18th-century wax anatomical models — beautiful, unsettling, and exactly the macabre-curiosity register the parents love. Newly reopened after restoration.

    Adults + older kids · msn.unifi.it
  9. 9
    Mercato Centrale + San Lorenzo + gelato crawl
    food hall · leather market · Grey's mission

    Graze the upstairs food hall, browse the San Lorenzo leather stalls, and run a proper gelato crawl (Vivoli, Perché No!, Gelateria della Passera). Grey's favorite research project.

    Family · mercatocentrale.com
  10. 10
    A day trip out of the city
    Lucca · Pisa · Fiesole · a Tuscan farm

    Bike the walls of Lucca, climb the leaning tower at Pisa, or ride up to Fiesole for Etruscan-Roman ruins and hilltop views. Or a Tuscan farm/cook day in the countryside — all easy by train from Florence.

    All ages · day trips by rail

Bargello sculpture museum

Masaccio's frescoes that launched the Renaissance, then wander the artisan quarter across the river and land at a Santo Spirito aperitivo as the square fills up. The grown-ups' perfect afternoon.

— Weird, wacky & unforgettable

Offbeat & hidden gems

The strange, memorable, off-the-tourist-track finds the boys will actually talk about — vetted, real, and doable from our Oltrarno base. (Galileo's finger, La Specola's wax anatomy and the Palazzo Vecchio secret passages are already up in the Top 10 — these are the rest of the rabbit hole.)

Grey · Keir · Rhys · quirky food

Buchette del vino — the wine windows

Tiny arched hatches cut into medieval palazzo walls, built in the 1500s so noble families could sell wine tax-free straight to the street. Around 150 survive, and a handful are live again — order gelato, a spritz or (for Rhys) a glass of Chianti handed out through a hole in the wall. Hunt them on foot; Babae on Via Santo Spirito pours through its buchetta near our base.

All ages · free to spot, cheap to order · self-guided walk

buchettedelvino.org
Kei & parents · secret passage

The Vasari Corridor

The Medici's private elevated tunnel — three-quarters of a kilometer from Palazzo Vecchio, over the Ponte Vecchio, to Pitti Palace — so the Grand Duke never had to walk with the commoners. Sealed for eight years, it reopened in December 2024. Pure Escher/Voynich: a hidden corridor threaded through the city above everyone's heads.

Older kids + adults · book the Uffizi + corridor supplement well ahead · max 5 tickets

uffizi.it
Keir & Grey · lucky ritual

Il Porcellino — rub the boar's nose

A big bronze wild boar has slobbered water into the Mercato Nuovo since the 1600s. The ritual: put a coin in his mouth, let it drop through the grate for luck, and rub his snout — worn mirror-bright by centuries of hands — so you're guaranteed to come back to Florence. Two seconds, and Keir gets a superstition to obsess over.

All ages · free · a 30-second detour by the leather market

visittuscany.com
Jude · pigments & minerals

Zecchi — the Renaissance color shop

A tiny artists'-supply shop on Via dello Studio that still grinds pigments by hand from the 14th-century recipes of Cennino Cennini — including a real ultramarine blue made from ground lapis lazuli. Jars of raw mineral color, gold leaf and rabbit-skin glue. For Jude the rockhound-meets-engineer, it's where gemstones literally become paint.

All ages · free to browse · near the Duomo

zecchi.it
Kei & parents · alchemy / apothecary

Officina di Santa Maria Novella — world's oldest pharmacy

Dominican friars have been brewing elixirs, potions and perfumes here since 1221, and the frescoed sales rooms and old apothecary jars are still exactly where they were in the 1500s. Ancient recipes, painted vaults, a little museum in the old workshops — the alchemist's-cabinet energy the parents live for. Free to walk in and smell it all.

All ages · free entry (it's a working shop + museum) · by the train station

smnovella.com
Keir & parents · fantasy cave

The Buontalenti Grotto in Boboli

Hidden in the Boboli Gardens, a Mannerist fake cave dripping with fake stalactites, sponge-stone shepherds and animals melting out of the walls, with frescoes of fantastic beasts overhead — built for Francesco I de' Medici in the 1580s. Deliberately weird and dreamlike; peak Voynich. Entry is included with the Boboli ticket.

All ages · included with Boboli entry · timed grotto viewings

uffizi.it/boboli
Jude & parents · urban legend / ghost story

The window that can never be closed

On Piazza Santissima Annunziata, one shutter of Palazzo Grifoni has stood open for nearly 500 years. Legend says a young bride watched from it for a husband who never came home from war; when the family tried to close it after she died, books flew off shelves and furniture scraped across the floor until they reopened it. The bronze horseman in the square stares straight at it. Free to find, and genuinely eerie.

All ages · free · spot it from the piazza

visittuscany.com
Rhys (18) & parents · make-your-own

AquaFlor — build your own perfume

In a Renaissance palazzo near Santa Croce, a master perfumer walks you through 100-plus essences to blend and bottle a fragrance that's entirely your own. A proper grown-up, hands-on hour and a half — a sharp, un-touristy 18th-birthday experience for Rhys, and catnip for anyone who loved the old apothecary.

Best 12+ / adults · book ahead (daily 10:30 & 15:30) · take home a bottle

aquaflorexperience.com
