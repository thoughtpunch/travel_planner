— The one-paragraph version

What we landed on, in a nutshell

The old idea was nine places in 34 nights — packing and catching trains eight times, several mid-week. We reshaped it to eight stops built around longer bases and quiet-day moves, timed so Dan never travels on a workday and the kids actually unpack. The choppy old opening (Milan → Como → Cinque Terre) became a clean run down to the sea: Milan 2 nights → Turin 2 → the Ligurian coast at Chiavari 4. We dropped Lake Como, turned Bologna into a food day-trip, gave Venice a full week, and used the saved calm to go deeper everywhere.

The trade we made in one line We chose more rest, sea, and presence over more places. Same flights, same 34 nights, same big-ticket cities — we just fixed the pace.

— So nobody burns out (or gets bored)

The three gears

The whole point is balance — not go-go-go, not a sleepy blur. A good week has all three of these, and the reshape spreads them evenly instead of stacking exhausting days back to back.

🟢
Slow gear — coffee in a piazza, the kids loose in a garden or the surf, nobody on a schedule. Ligurian beach mornings · Boboli & Villa Borghese gardens · the Lido · café afternoons.
🔵
Wow gear — the cry-at-how-beautiful-this-is day. The Dolomites · Venice at dawn · Sacra di San Michele above the Susa Valley · the Cinque Terre coast.
🔴
Worth-it grind — crowded and tiring, but you'd regret skipping it. Vatican · Colosseum · Uffizi · St Mark's.
📵
Off-phones, in the world — the physical stuff kids remember. Swimming & boats on the coast · cable cars and mountain trails in the Dolomites.

— How week one shook out

Where we spend week one

This was the decision the whole reshape hinged on. We land jet-lagged in Milan on a Friday night, then run gently south to the sea over the first week (Dan's first PTO block) before heading to Florence. Rather than one single base, we chose a clean three-part opener — Milan 2 nights → Turin 2 → the coast at Chiavari 4. Here's the case for each, and the one we set aside.

In · 4 nights

The Ligurian coast

Chiavari · Cinque Terre by train · the sea
  • Our stretch with the sea — swimming (water's still ~22°C), boats, cliff trails
  • The anti-boredom answer: kids doing, not strolling
  • Camogli → San Fruttuoso boat + Cinque Terre hops, all by train
  • Carries Rhys's 18th (Sep 18) — seafood over the Mediterranean
  • Cinque Terre villages get crowded (we base in calmer, cheaper Chiavari)
In · where we land

Milan

Arrival base · big-city taste
  • Where we land — two nights to shake off the jet lag
  • Loads for teens: Duomo rooftop, bone chapel, Galleria, football
  • Frecciarossa straight on to Turin (~1h)
  • Kept short on purpose — it's the doorway, not the destination
In · 2 nights

Turin

Two knockouts · Alps gateway
  • Sacra di San Michele — a 1,000-year abbey-fortress on a mountaintop
  • Mole + Cinema Museum (glass elevator up the hollow) & the Museo Egizio

We already get water on the Ligurian coast and mountain drama in the Dolomites, so Como's unique offering was the least kid-relevant part. Dropped from the loop; worth a day-trip from Milan someday.
— Because it kept coming up

Why Lake Como didn't make the cut

It's beautiful and worth seeing — but the thing that makes it famous turned out not to be an us thing. Here's the honest breakdown that put it to bed.

Why it's so popular

World-class scenery + belle-époque villa gardens (Villa del Balbianello — they filmed Star Wars & Bond here) + celebrity/honeymoon glamour + it's an hour from Milan. The essence: gaze at the water, tour a garden, take a boat, sip a spritz.

Why maybe not our week

Its draw is romantic and aesthetic, not active — low on things for the boys to do. And we already get mountain drama (Dolomites) and water (Liguria) elsewhere, so Como's unique offering is the glamour, which is the least kid-relevant part.

The verdict Worth a day, not a week — and honestly, maybe a future couples trip. A single day-trip from Milan (train + boat to Villa del Balbianello) would deliver 90% of it; as a base with teenagers it'd struggle. So Como came out, and the opener runs Milan → Turin → the coast at Chiavari instead.
— The four we built around

The anchors

These four never wavered — they're the heart of the trip. Three earn a full week; the Dolomites close it out.

Florence · 7 nights

Renaissance base. Duomo, Uffizi, artisan workshops — and the Bologna food day-trip (37 min by Frecciarossa) is there when we want it.

Rome · 7 nights

Two moods in one city — ancient (Colosseum, Forum, Pantheon) and the Vatican. A week barely covers it, and Villa Borghese gives the kids room to run.

Venice / Lido · 7 nights

The reason for the whole east side — MuMu on the Lido. A full week means she's unhurried, Padua isn't rushed, and our anniversary (Oct 9) gets the canals.

Dolomites · the finale

An apartment in Laion above Ponte Gardena, our one rental-car leg, and the face-melting Alps. Carries Keir's bike ride and Grey's 12th (Oct 12) — cable cars, the Ortisei railway trail, rifugio lunches, a mountain birthday.
