Bologna — la grassa

Oct 2–4 (2 nts)The food weekendTrain to Venice next

Two nights, one job: eat. "La Grassa" — the Fat One — is where tortellini and tagliatelle al ragù were invented, roofed by 40 km of UNESCO porticoes so it rains and you never get wet. A hands-on pasta class, a market crawl through the Quadrilatero, a tower to climb, and a wunderkammer of anatomical waxes for the Escher/Voynich-minded. Dan's on calls 4–11pm, so this is a mornings-and-lunch town — which is exactly when Bologna eats best.

— The shortlist

Bologna Top 10

Two days, food first. This is a weekend, not a week — pick the meals, walk them off under the porticoes, and climb one tower. EU under-18s are free at state sites.

  1. 1
    Hands-on fresh-pasta class
    central Bologna · book ahead

    Learn to hand-roll the sfoglia and fold tortellini and tagliatelle — the region's signature skill, with a station for every kid. Try La Vecchia Scuola Bolognese, or a home class through Cesarine. Sells out; reserve early.

    All ages · English · lavecchiascuola.com · cesarine.com
  2. 2
    Quadrilatero market food crawl
    the medieval market lanes off Piazza Maggiore

    Graze the old market grid: cured meats and tortelloni at Tamburini, a stand-up lunch in the Mercato di Mezzo, and a glass at Osteria del Sole. This is the meal you'll talk about later.

    All ages · tamburini.com
  3. 3
    The Two Towers — climb Asinelli
    Piazza di Porta Ravegnana · timed tickets

    498 wooden steps up the leaning Asinelli tower for the best view over the red rooftops, then out to Piazza Maggiore and the Neptune fountain. Small time-slots — book ahead online.

    Age 8+ for the climb · torreasinelli.it
  4. 4
    The porticoes — walk to San Luca
    UNESCO · 3.8 km covered arcade

    Bologna's porticoes are a World Heritage site; the longest, Portico di San Luca, climbs 666 arches to a hilltop basilica with a sweeping view. Walk it, or ride the little San Luca Express tourist train up and stroll down.

    All ages · bolognawelcome.com
  5. 5
    The feast — ragù, tortellini in brodo, mortadella
    a proper sit-down osteria

    The holy trinity done right: tagliatelle al ragù (never "spaghetti bolognese," which doesn't exist here), tortellini in brodo, and a plate of real mortadella. Book one good osteria for the birthday-worthy dinner.

    All ages · bolognawelcome.com
  6. 6
    Palazzo Poggi — the university's wunderkammer
    via Zamboni · Bologna University museums

    18th-century anatomical wax models, obstetric teaching figures, ship models, maps and natural-history oddities in a frescoed palace. A genuine cabinet of curiosities — pure Escher/Voynich energy.

    Adults + older kids · English · sma.unibo.it
  7. 7
    Food park or a Parmigiano + balsamic day
    Grand Tour Italia (edge of town) · or toward Modena

    Option A: the giant Grand Tour Italia food park (the old FICO) — pizza and gelato made in front of you (re-check opening days). Option B: a morning trip toward Modena to watch Parmigiano-Reggiano made at dawn and taste barrel-aged balsamic.

    All ages · grandtouritalia.it · parmigianoreggiano.com
  8. 8
    Gelato + Santo Stefano's "seven churches"
    Piazza Santo Stefano

    A cone from a top gelateria, then lose yourselves in the Basilica di Santo Stefano — a maze of seven interlinked churches and courtyards stacked up over centuries. Quiet, strange, and free.

    All ages · free · bolognawelcome.com
  9. 9
    Archiginnasio + the Anatomical Theatre
    next to Piazza Maggiore

    The old university hall, its walls crusted with carved student coats-of-arms, hides a wooden dissection theatre — tiered benches around a marble slab, watched over by skinned wooden figures. Deeply on-brand.

    All ages · archiginnasio.it
  10. 10
    Motor Valley — optional, for Jude
    Ducati in-city · Ferrari/Lamborghini toward Modena

    Ferrari, Lamborghini and Ducati museums all sit in this corner of Italy — the car kid's dream. Honest heads-up: two nights is tight to add a factory day, so treat it as a maybe, or catch it another leg.

    Age 6+ for factory tours · English · Jude's page

Deeper cuts

Grown-ups love

Osteria del Sole

A 1465 wine-only bar hidden in the Quadrilatero: they pour the wine, you bring the food from the market stalls around it. Buy mortadella and cheese, sit at a shared table, stay all afternoon.

osteriadelsole.it

A Modena food morning

Half an hour by train: traditional balsamic acetaias in family attics, a Parmigiano dairy at dawn, and Massimo Bottura's home city. The grown-up food pilgrimage of the region.

visitmodena.it

Basilica di Santo Stefano

Beyond the family visit, it rewards a slow adult wander — the "seven churches" layer Roman columns, a Romanesque courtyard, and an early replica of Jerusalem's Holy Sepulchre. Meditative and nearly empty.

— Weird, wacky & unforgettable

Offbeat & hidden gems

The strange, memorable, off-the-tourist-track finds the boys will actually talk about — vetted, real, and doable from our central base.

Keir · secret canal window

La Finestrella di Via Piella

A little shuttered window in a plain wall on Via Piella opens onto a hidden canal running between the houses — Bologna's "Little Venice," the last uncovered stretch of the medieval waterways buried under the city. Push the shutter, peek through, and the street turns into Venice. Grab a gelato nearby and make a game of finding it.

All ages · free · Via Piella 16, central, walkable

bolognawelcome.com
Keir + Grey · acoustic trick

The whispering telephone

Under the vaulted Voltone del Podestà, next to Piazza Maggiore, two people stand at opposite diagonal corners, face the stone, and whisper — and hear each other perfectly across the noisy square, as if on a phone. A medieval acoustic accident (rumoured once used to hear lepers' confessions). Split up, whisper a password, watch the 9-year-old's face.

All ages · free · Piazza Maggiore, central

bolognawelcome.com
Rhys · cheeky optical illusion

Neptune's not-a-finger

Stand at the exact spot by the Sala Borsa entrance and look at the giant bronze Neptune's raised left hand — the sculptor Giambologna reportedly angled his thumb so that, from this one viewpoint, it turns into something distinctly ruder. A 450-year-old visual dirty joke hiding in the main square. Edgy, funny, and completely real.

Teens + adults · free · Piazza del Nettuno, central

bolognawelcome.com
Jude · medieval legend made real

The three arrows of Corte Isolani

Look straight up in the dark medieval portico at Strada Maggiore 19 and you'll spot three ancient wooden arrows lodged in the ceiling beams. Legend says assassins fired at a nobleman, missed when a naked woman appeared at a window to distract them, and the arrows stuck there forever. Grisly little mystery, hidden in plain sight.

All ages · free · Strada Maggiore 19, central

bolognawelcome.com
Parents · astronomy + jealousy legend

Cassini's sundial & the unfinished façade

Inside San Petronio, a brass line runs 67 m across the floor — the world's longest indoor meridian, built by astronomer Cassini in 1655; a pinhole 27 m up drops a disc of sunlight that crawls along it at solar noon. The basilica was meant to out-scale St. Peter's in Rome until (legend says) a jealous Pope had the money diverted — which is why its front is still half bare brick. Pure Escher/Voynich.

All ages · free (noon for the sun-disc) · Piazza Maggiore

atlasobscura.com
Rhys + parents · haunting art

Museo per la Memoria di Ustica

A hushed hall in Bolognina rebuilt around the actual wreckage of a DC-9 shot from the sky in 1980, wrapped in Christian Boltanski's installation: 81 slow-pulsing lights and 81 mirrors for the 81 victims, with hidden speakers murmuring their imagined last thoughts. Devastating, beautiful, unforgettable — heavy, so best for the older boys and adults.

Teens + adults · free · Fri–Sun 17:00–20:00 · 10-min walk from Centrale (our base)

museomemoriaustica.it
Grey · gelato, but a museum

Gelato Museum Carpigiani

The world's first museum of gelato — 20-plus antique churning machines, the history and science of how it's frozen, and hands-on tasting workshops where you actually make a batch. A short hop out to Anzola (bus 87 or car), free entry, workshops booked ahead. Grey's whole trip in one building.

All ages · free museum (workshops paid, book ahead) · Anzola, ~20 min by bus/car

bolognawelcome.com
Grey · the food secret

The tortellino & Venus's navel

Bolognese legend says the tortellino was shaped by an innkeeper who spied the goddess Venus through a keyhole and modelled the little pasta ring on her navel — which is why locals defend its exact fold so fiercely. Order tortellini in brodo, know the story, and settle the family debate over who folds the best one after the pasta class.

All ages · at any proper osteria, central
