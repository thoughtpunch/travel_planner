Milan — the sprint start

Sep 11–13 (2 nts)Arrive MXP 9:55pm FriThen train to Turin

We land late Friday, so this is a jetlag-soft landing: really one full day (Saturday) plus a Sunday morning before the Turin train — a gentle, punchy opener. Go easy. Priorities if the clock is tight: the Leonardo science museum with its walk-through Enrico Toti submarine, the Duomo rooftop, the bone chapel of San Bernardino, and Rhys's first legal aperitivo on the Navigli canals. Bergamo's funicular old town is an easy optional half-day if energy allows.

— The shortlist

Milan Top 10

Ranked for ~1.5 days with a 9-year-old pace-setter and two parents who love the weird-medieval and the botanical. EU under-18s are free at Italian state museums, so the boys walk into most of these free. Dan works 4–11pm, so mornings and early afternoons are family time.

  1. 1
    The Last Supper / Cenacolo Vinciano
    Santa Maria delle Grazie · timed 15-min slots

    Leonardo's fading masterpiece, seen in a hushed 15-minute window. It sells out weeks — sometimes months — ahead, so book the instant your dates are firm. Non-negotiable if you got a slot.

    All ages · English · cenacolovinciano.org
  2. 2
    Duomo + rooftop terraces
    Piazza del Duomo · climb the stairs or lift

    Walk up among the spires and flying buttresses with the Alps on the horizon — the best thing you'll do all day. Book a timed rooftop ticket ahead to skip the long line.

    All ages · English · duomomilano.it
  3. 3
    San Bernardino alle Ossa (bone chapel)
    near Piazza Santo Stefano · free

    A small side chapel walled floor-to-ceiling in human skulls and bones — macabre, beautiful, and exactly the Escher/Voynich aesthetic. A quick, unforgettable stop; gauge a sensitive 9-year-old.

    Adults + older kids · free
  4. 4
    Navigli canals aperitivo
    Naviglio Grande · early evening

    Leonardo helped engineer these canals; now the towpaths are Milan's best evening scene. Rhys is 18 — this is his first legal Italian aperitivo, a spritz and a spread of nibbles by the water.

    All ages · 18+ to drink · free to wander
  5. 5
    Museo Nazionale Scienza e Tecnologia Leonardo da Vinci
    Sant'Ambrogio · huge, hands-on

    Italy's biggest science-and-tech museum: Leonardo's machine models, steam trains, planes, and the walk-through Enrico Toti submarine. The single best rainy-day or high-energy stop for the boys.

    All ages · English · museoscienza.org
  6. 6
    Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II + the bull-heel spin
    between Duomo and La Scala · free

    The glittering 1877 iron-and-glass arcade. Find the mosaic bull on the floor and spin on your heel in the little divot for good luck — a required, silly family photo.

    All ages · free
  7. 7
    Sforza Castle + Michelangelo's Rondanini Pietà
    Castello Sforzesco · courtyards free

    A proper brick fortress with battlements and moats for the boys, plus Michelangelo's haunting last, unfinished Pietà inside. The courtyards and ramparts are free; the museums are free for under-18s.

    All ages · English · milanocastello.it
  8. 8
    Duomo archaeological area + Battistero below
    under the Duomo · combo ticket

    Descend beneath the cathedral to the 4th-century baptistery and Roman ruins where Milan was Christianized. An eerie, quiet underground layer that pairs perfectly with the rooftop.

    All ages · English · duomomilano.it
  9. 9
    Brera district + Orto Botanico di Brera
    Brera · botanic garden free

    Milan's arty village of cobbled lanes and galleries, wrapped around a hidden 18th-century botanical garden with ancient ginkgos — Kei's kind of green pocket in the middle of the city.

    All ages · English · ortibotanici.unimi.it
  10. 10
    Classic Milanese food crawl
    trattorias citywide

    The golden saffron risotto alla milanese, breaded cotoletta, slow-braised ossobuco, and a slice of panettone. Milan invented all four — build a lunch or dinner around them.

    All ages · trattorias & markets
— More to do

Deeper cuts

Grown-ups love

Brera Pinacoteca (or skip)

Raphael, Caravaggio and Mantegna's foreshortened dead Christ in one grand gallery. Per the family "one major art hit" rule, do it only if you're craving painting — otherwise save your art appetite for Florence.

pinacotecabrera.org

Cimitero Monumentale

An open-air museum of Art Nouveau and Symbolist tombs — melting bronze figures, miniature temples, impossible staircases. Free, uncrowded, and deeply Escher-adjacent.

monumentale.comune.milano.it

Aperitivo culture

Beyond the Navigli: Brera's terraces and the Camparino in Galleria, birthplace of the Negroni-adjacent Milanese ritual. One good spritz-and-snacks stop is the city in a glass.

camparino.com

Family wins

The Toti submarine

Walk through a real Italian navy submarine parked inside the Leonardo science museum — cramped bunks, torpedo room, the works. Reliably the boys' favorite ten minutes in Milan.

museoscienza.org

Duomo rooftop

Kids love being up among the gargoyles and spires more than any museum. Take the lift up, walk the terraces, take the stairs down if legs allow.

duomomilano.it

Gelato + the Galleria

A cone from a good gelateria, the bull-heel spin, and a lap of the glittering arcade. Cheap, easy, and exactly the low-key win a jet-lagged arrival day needs.

yesmilano.it
— Weird, wacky & unforgettable

Offbeat & hidden gems

The strange, memorable, off-the-tourist-track finds the boys will actually talk about — vetted, real, and doable from our base near Milano Centrale. The bone chapel is already in the Top 10; this is the rest of Milan's weird streak.

Rhys + parents · rooftop secret

Highline Galleria skywalk

A 250-metre catwalk along the rooftops of the Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II, 40 metres above the shoppers, popping out over Piazza del Duomo. Reopened February 2026 after a restoration — a totally different, vertiginous take on the arcade you'll cross at ground level. Timed tickets, book ahead.

All ages who can handle heights · timed entry from ~€15 · steps involved

highlinegalleria.it
Jude + Keir · rocks, gore & giants

Museo di Storia Naturale

Milan's cathedral-like natural history museum: a hall of minerals and gems for Jude's rockhounding eye, a Spinosaurus snout, a cast of "Stan" the T-rex, pygmy-elephant skeletons, and rooms of Victorian taxidermy dioramas — pure Escher/Voynich wunderkammer for the parents. Free for under-18s.

All ages · Porta Venezia, walkable/metro · free for EU under-18s

museodistorianaturalemilano.it
parents + Jude · a house frozen in 1890

Bagatti Valsecchi house-museum

Two 19th-century baron brothers built a Neo-Renaissance fantasy home and crammed 17 rooms with armour, curiosities, secret compartments and hidden modern plumbing disguised as antiques — one of Europe's best-preserved house-museums. A quiet, strange, design-nerd gem off the Montenapoleone shopping drag.

All ages · central (Montenapoleone) · open Tue–Sun afternoons

museobagattivalsecchi.org
Jude + Keir · gross-cool history

WWII air-raid shelter, Piazza Grandi

Descend into a restored 1936 bomb shelter — 25 rooms that hid up to 450 terrified civilians, walls still scrawled with their messages. The city runs a free, roughly hour-long guided tour; it's raw, eerie and unforgettable. Book the slot in advance on the municipal page.

Older kids + up (dark, heavy theme) · free · advance booking required

comune.milano.it
Keir + Jude · engineering + a view

Branca Tower

A skeletal 108-metre steel tower that Gio Ponti threw up in Parco Sempione in just ten weeks back in 1933. A glass lift shoots you to a tiny belvedere in under a minute for a view that can reach the Alps — far weirder and emptier than the Duomo roof, and Keir gets his one big up-high thing.

All ages · Parco Sempione · check seasonal opening days/hours

yesmilano.it
Jude · cars, short trip out

Museo Alfa Romeo, Arese

The official Alfa Romeo museum on the old factory site: six floors of racing legends, concept cars and gleaming engines, exactly Jude's wheelhouse. Reachable carless — M1 metro to Rho Fiera, then the Z120 bus to the door. Plan the return bus times, especially on a Sunday.

All ages, car-obsessed teens especially · ~45–60 min each way by metro + bus

museoalfaromeo.com
Grey + Rhys · playful & photogenic

Bar Luce at Fondazione Prada

Wes Anderson designed this café down to the pastel terrazzo, jukebox and two custom pinball machines — a walk-into-a-movie spot Grey and Rhys will love. It sits inside Fondazione Prada's art complex, where a gold-leaf "Haunted House" tower glints over the courtyard. Café is free to enter; grab a hot chocolate.

All ages · Metro M3 Lodi T.I.B.B. · café free, gallery ticketed

fondazioneprada.org
Grey · food oddity, always a line

Panzerotti at Luini

A tiny takeaway steps behind the Duomo that has fried one thing since 1949: the panzerotto, a half-moon of dough pocketing molten mozzarella and tomato. There's no seating and always a local queue out the door — cheap, greasy, and Grey's kind of hands-on street-food win.

All ages · behind the Duomo · a couple of euros, cash-fast

luini.it
