Housing — whole-place, weekly, carless

34 nights · Sep 11–Oct 15 Family of 6 · sleeps-6 apartments Belt over center · no car except the Dolomites

Nine bases, one rule: book the whole place, take the weekly rate on the long stays, and never sleep somewhere the trains don't reach. We're carless the entire loop except the Dolomites, so every base has to be walkable to a station, ferry, funicular or metro. "Belt over center" where it helps — a bigger, calmer, cheaper apartment one stop out that's still on the line. The one deliberate splurge is the Val Gardena chalet in the Dolomites, the single leg where we actually have a rental car. Whole thing lands around €8–11K.

— The whole strategy in three lines

Three rules that book every base

Get these right and the housing line more or less takes care of itself. Everything below is just applying them, stop by stop.

01 · Whole place, always

Six people means one 3-bedroom-plus apartment, never a clutch of hotel rooms. A kitchen turns four or five nights a week into groceries instead of restaurant checks, and a living room is where a 9-, 12-, 16- and 18-year-old actually decompress. Whole-place is the single biggest cost and comfort lever.

02 · Belt over center

Where the postcard center is cramped and dear, book the bigger, quieter apartment one stop out that's still on the line — Oltrarno instead of the Duomo scrum, Garbatella instead of the centro, the Lido instead of San Marco. More room, better value, more local, zero loss of access.

03 · On the line, or don't book it

Carless means every base must be walkable to rail, ferry, funicular or metro. A dreamy village off the line is a trap without a car. So: Como town, not a lake hamlet. Levanto on the Cinque Terre line, not a cliff village. The only exception is the Dolomites, where we have the car.

— Where the savings actually come from

Long stays earn the weekly rate

Two of our nine legs are seven nights — Florence and Rome, the two work bases. Those are exactly where the discount lives.

Book 7+ nights and the nightly price drops Airbnb and Booking both apply a weekly discount at 7 nights — typically 10–20% off the nightly rate, and shoulder season (mid-Sep to mid-Oct) is already off peak. Our Florence (7n) and Rome (7n) bases each clear the threshold, and the Lido (5n) and Val Gardena (5n) get most of the way there. The short 2–3 night legs won't — so on those, convenience beats a rate you can't unlock anyway.
The short-leg exception On Milan (2n), Como (3n), Cinque Terre (2n) and Bologna (2n) there's no meaningful weekly rate to chase, so optimize for position instead: next to the station you arrive at and leave from. A €20/night premium to be a five-minute walk from Milano Centrale is worth it when you land at 9:55pm and catch a train to Como the next morning.
