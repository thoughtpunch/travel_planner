— The whole strategy in three lines

Three rules that book every base

Get these right and the housing line more or less takes care of itself. Everything below is just applying them, stop by stop.

01 · Whole place, always

Six people means one whole apartment that sleeps all six in its bedrooms, never a clutch of hotel rooms. Two bedrooms is enough if the second holds bunks — and filtering to three empties out the search. A kitchen turns four or five nights a week into groceries instead of restaurant checks, and a living room is where a 9-, 12-, 16- and 18-year-old actually decompress. Whole-place is the single biggest cost and comfort lever.

02 · Belt over center

Where the postcard center is cramped and dear, book the bigger, quieter apartment one stop out that's still on the line — Oltrarno instead of the Duomo scrum, Garbatella instead of the centro, the Lido instead of San Marco. More room, better value, more local, zero loss of access.

03 · On the line, or don't book it

Carless means every base must be walkable to rail, ferry, funicular or metro. A dreamy village off the line is a trap without a car. So: Turin by Porta Nuova, not a hill town. Chiavari on the main-line coast, not a Cinque Terre cliff village. The only exception is the Dolomites, where we have the car.

— Where the savings actually come from

Long stays earn the weekly rate

Three of our eight legs are seven nights — Florence, Rome and Venice/Lido. Those are exactly where the weekly discount lives. Florence and Rome are the true work bases (settle in, desk, routine); the Lido is a seven-night stay built around MuMu — but Oct 5–9 are Mon–Fri, so Dan still works those evenings and the wifi test applies there too.

Book 7+ nights and the nightly price drops Airbnb and Booking both apply a weekly discount at 7 nights — typically 10–20% off the nightly rate, and shoulder season (mid-Sep to mid-Oct) is already off peak. Our Florence (7n), Rome (7n) and Venice/Lido (7n) bases each clear the threshold, and the Chiavari (4n) and Kastelruth (4n) legs get part of the way there. The short 2-night legs won't — so on those, convenience beats a rate you can't unlock anyway.
The short-leg exception On Milan (2n) and Turin (2n) there's no meaningful weekly rate to chase, so optimize for position instead: next to the station you arrive at and leave from. A €20/night premium to be a five-minute walk from Milano Centrale is worth it when you land at 9:55pm and catch a train to Turin the next morning.

— The cheaper, less-touristy pick in each long stay

Neighborhood playbook

For the week-long bases, this is where the value actually hides — real residential quarters off the tourist strip, every one still on a tram, metro or regionale line. Willing to pay a 30–45 min transit tax? The bottom line of each card opens even cheaper ground.

🎨 Florence · Sep 19–26

Belt the Duomo scrum — all on tram, bus or local rail.

  • Campo di Marte — leafy residential east; bus/tram ~15 min in, very local
  • Rifredi — regionale 10 min to S.M.N. + tram T2; cheap, unpretentious
  • Novoli — tram T2 straight to the center and the airport; strong apartment value
  • Oltrarno / San Frediano — artisan south bank; walkable, cooler + cheaper than the north-bank strip
  • Transit tax: Scandicci (tram T1 direct) or Sesto/Prato (regionale, much cheaper)

🏛️ Rome · Sep 26–Oct 3

Off the Centro Storico — all on metro or tram.

  • Garbatella — 1920s garden district, metro B; authentic, family-friendly, great value
  • Pigneto — hip, foodie, less touristy; tram + metro C (a teen win)
  • San Giovanni — metro A; residential, walkable-ish to the Colosseum
  • San Lorenzo — university quarter, gritty-cool, cheap, by Termini and the trams
  • Transit tax: EUR (metro B, modern & roomy) or an FL-regional town

🚤 Venice · Oct 3–10

MuMu's on the Lido — already the value + space play.

  • Lido di Venezia — beaches + real family apartments + vaporetto; the pick (near MuMu), cheapest-per-space
  • Cannaregio — the most local sestiere in Venice; cheaper than San Marco, great cicchetti
  • Giudecca — quiet, big value, a quick vaporetto across
  • Transit tax: Mestre (mainland) — far cheaper, 10 min by train/tram into Venice
And the shorter legs Chiavari → if it's full or dear, hop one stop to Lavagna (long sandy beach, cheaper). Turin → San Salvario or Vanchiglia by Porta Nuova are hip and affordable. Milan → the Porta Venezia / Loreto belt on the metro beats the Centrale-front premium if value wins over the late-night convenience. Dolomites → settled: the Singerhof in Kastelruth, which is exactly this play — the cheaper village one ridge over, with better lift access than the famous one.
