Housing — whole-place, weekly, carless

34 nights · Sep 11–Oct 15 Family of 6 · sleeps-6 apartments Belt over center · no car except the Dolomites

Eight bases, one rule: book the whole place, take the weekly rate on the long stays, and never sleep somewhere the trains don't reach. We're carless the entire loop except the Dolomites, so every base has to be walkable to a station, ferry, funicular or metro. "Belt over center" where it helps — a bigger, calmer, cheaper apartment one stop out that's still on the line. The one leg that bends the rule is the Dolomites, where we have a rental car and are staying at a farmhouse in Kastelruth that no train reaches. All eight are booked. Every one of the 34 nights is paid for, and the whole thing landed at $8,930 — $263 a night for six of us, whole-place.

— Why these eight, in hindsight

The three rules that picked every base

Kept as the record of why each base is what it is — not as instructions. Every bed is booked and paid; nothing here is a decision any more.

The rules we actually applied 01 · Whole place, always. Six people means one apartment sleeping all six in its bedrooms, never a clutch of hotel rooms. Two bedrooms was enough wherever the second held bunks; filtering to three emptied out most cities.

02 · Weekly rate on the long legs. Three of the eight legs are seven nights — Florence, Rome and Venice/Lido — and both Airbnb and Booking drop the nightly price 10–20% at seven. Shoulder season did the rest. That discount is already baked into the paid totals below.

03 · Never sleep where the trains don't reach. Carless for the whole loop except the Dolomites, so every base had to be walkable to a station, ferry, funicular or metro. The one base that bends the rule — the Singerhof — bends it on purpose, because that is the leg with the rental car.

The one that bends the rule — on purpose · ✓ booked The Dolomites leg is the one no train reaches, and that's fine because it's the one leg with the rental car (collected at Venice Marco Polo Oct 10, dropped at Malpensa noon Oct 15). Booked: the Singerhof, St. Valentin 4, Kastelruth — a farmhouse in the hamlet between Seis am Schlern and Kastelruth, under the Schlern. $1,004 for 4 nights, against a $1,400 estimate.

Not Val Gardena, deliberately. Being on the Seiser Alm side puts the Alpe di Siusi cableway 8 minutes from the door (it was 25 from Ortisei), Bolzano and Ötzi at 30, and the Völs hay bath at 10 — while pushing Seceda and the Sella passes about 20 minutes further out. It also settles the off-street parking filter outright: it's rural, so the village ZTLs never come up. It carries Grey's 12th birthday (Oct 12) and Keir's bike day (Oct 11).
