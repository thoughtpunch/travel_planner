Strategy · Housing

Housing — bigger, cheaper, carless

Cap: $1,500 / week Sleeps 6–8 (room for grandma) Belt-over-center · no car

Housing is the one line that can blow the whole $35K. The play: never book the center or the island — book the walkable, transit-connected belt town one stop out, take weekly/monthly rates, and put the long stays where houses are cheap. That swings a $20K+ housing fear down to roughly $12–15K — and buys one beautiful countryside manse in the middle of it.

— How the number comes down

Three levers that beat everything else

These matter more than any single listing. Get all three right and the belt strategy basically pays for the farmhouse.

01 · Reallocate the nights

Housing cost is wildly uneven by region. Sicily, the Garfagnana and Bologna's belt are half the price of central Rome or the Lido for the same size house. Spend the long stays where houses are cheap and keep the pricey legs tight.

02 · Belt over center

Always pick a town on a regional rail line, 15–25 min out — not a pretty village off it. On-the-line = walkable + fully carless. Off-the-line = trapped without a car. This single rule is the whole game.

03 · Monthly rates

Booking 28+ nights unlocks Airbnb/Booking monthly rates — often 30–50% off nightly. Our 2–3 week bases already earn weekly discounts; nudging Bologna and Venice toward 28 nights unlocks the big one. Sept–Nov is low season on top.

— The trap to avoid

There are two totally different price markets

When you search "Tuscan farmhouse sleeps 8 with pool," you land on curated-luxury broker sites. Their prices are not our market — and it's easy to conclude the whole idea is unaffordable when it isn't.

Tier 1 — Luxury brokers · skip entirely The Thinking Traveller, SopranoVillas, Salogi, SpecialItaly quote €4,000–13,000 / week for a farmhouse-with-pool sleeping 8. Beautiful, curated, and a completely different customer. Every "villa" that shows up first is priced for them, not us.
Tier 2 — Where we actually shop Direct-with-owner agriturismo (agriturismo.net, agriturismo.it) and Airbnb / Vrbo at monthly rates. Here a whole casale sleeping 8 with a pool runs ~€1,200–2,000 / week in shoulder season — and under €1,500 is genuinely reachable if you skip the two most-famous zones (Chianti core, Val d'Orcia) for a less-hyped valley.

— The one leg we go rural

The farmhouse: Garfagnana, above Lucca

The "cool old beautiful manse in the countryside." A true hills casale is the one place the carless rule bends — so this is our deliberate car-light week: a stone farmhouse in the Serchio valley with a regional-rail lifeline and village taxis, an hour off Lucca/Pisa. Slot it as the Tuscany slice of the Bologna block.

Why the Garfagnana

Medieval stone villages (Barga, Coreglia, Castelnuovo), chestnut forests and mountains, genuinely cheap, and — unusually for hill country — a working train line: the Lucca–Aulla "Garfagnana" regional. Barga is a walkable medieval town with a big Anglo-Italian community, so English is everywhere. Perfect for the family cooking + wood-fired pizza classes.

The rail lifeline

Stations at Bagni di Lucca, Fornaci di Barga, Barga-Gallicano, Castelnuovo and Piazza al Serchio. Lucca → Barga ~50 min, roughly hourly. From Lucca: Pisa ~30 min, the Viareggio coast ~25 min, Florence ~1h20. Book a place near a station and you barely need the taxi.

Timing catch — pools close ~October 1 Most Garfagnana/Tuscan pools run late May–late Sept (one listing: "May 4–Sept 28"). Our Bologna block is mostly October, when pools are shut. To swim: (a) pick a solar/heated pool, (b) pull the farmhouse week into mid/late September, or (c) skip the pool and lean on Bagni di Lucca's thermal spa instead. If a working pool is non-negotiable, the Sicily masseria (late Sept, warm) is the better home for it — see below.
