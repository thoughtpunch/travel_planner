Turin — arcades, cinema & a mountaintop abbey

Sep 13–15 (2 nts)PTO day #1Then coast via Genoa

An elegant, under-touristed café-and-arcade city that most trips skip — Italy's first capital, the birthplace of aperitivo, cinema and gianduja chocolate, with the Alps on the doorstep. Two nights, and two knockouts carry it: a 1,000-year-old abbey-fortress on a mountaintop, and a glass elevator riding up through the hollow spire of the Mole. We deliberately keep it light — no museum death-march.

— The shortlist

Turin in two knockouts (and the good rest)

Ranked for two nights with a 9-year-old pace-setter, three older boys and two parents who love the weird, the engineered and the well-made. Two big things carry the stop; everything else is café-and-arcade drifting. EU under-18s are free at Italian state museums, so the boys walk into most of these free.

  1. 1
    Sacra di San Michele — mountaintop abbey-fortress
    Avigliana + seasonal shuttle · aim for Sunday

    A 1,000-year-old abbey clamped to a rock spike 1,000 m over the Susa Valley — the monument that inspired The Name of the Rose. Climb the Staircase of the Dead into a church built on the summit, with the whole Alpine valley falling away below. The single best thing we'll do in Piedmont. Getting up: train to Sant'Ambrogio, then a shuttle up to the abbey (or hike the old Via Sacra if you want the climb).

    All ages · a real climb · sacradisanmichele.com
  2. 2
    Mole Antonelliana + National Cinema Museum
    the glass panoramic elevator · 85 m up

    Turin's spike of a landmark holds Italy's national cinema museum — movie sets, magic lanterns, optical illusions and props spiralling up the hollow interior — and a glass elevator that shoots straight up through the void to a terrace 85 m over the rooftops with the Alps behind. Teens and Keir both win.

    All ages · museocinema.it
  3. 3
    Museo Egizio — the great Egyptian museum
    via Accademia delle Scienze · book timed

    The world's oldest Egyptian museum and the most important outside Cairo: mummies, painted sarcophagi, an intact tomb and rooms of ~4,000-year-old everyday objects. Genuinely gripping for every age — the one indoor big-hitter worth the ticket. Under-18s free.

    All ages · museoegizio.it
  4. 4
    Aperitivo — where it was invented
    Piazza Vittorio Veneto · early evening

    Turin invented vermouth and, with it, the aperitivo ritual — a spritz or vermouth and a spread of free nibbles as the arcades light up. Newly-18 Rhys gets his first proper legal one here, in the city that started the whole thing. The kids get the snacks.

    All ages · 18+ to drink · free to wander
  5. 5
    Bicerin — the 260-year-old drink
    Caffè Al Bicerin, Piazza della Consolata · since 1763

    A little glass of layered espresso, drinking chocolate and cream that you do not stir — invented in Turin and served the same way since 1763. Warm, rich and theatrical: Grey's kind of edible win, and a perfect morning stop before a museum.

    All ages · Grey win · bicerin.it
  6. 6
    Gianduiotto & the chocolate crawl
    historic caffè-cioccolaterie · citywide

    Turin married chocolate to local hazelnuts and invented gianduja — the little gold-wrapped gianduiotto (1865) that became Nutella's ancestor. Graze the historic chocolatiers under the arcades; it's a low-effort, high-reward hour for the whole crew.

    Family · caffè & cioccolaterie
  7. 7
    The arcades + Piazza San Carlo café city
    18 km of porticoes · Turin's living room

    Turin has roughly 18 km of grand covered arcades — you can cross the centre in the rain without an umbrella — strung between baroque squares. Drift Via Roma to Piazza San Carlo (the "drawing room of Turin"), a gelato in hand, and let the elegant, uncrowded city do the work.

    All ages · free to wander
— A deliberate skip

The car museums we're passing on

Turin is Italy's Detroit — home of Fiat, and of two serious car temples. With only two nights and a car-obsessed 16-year-old, it's tempting. Dan's call: skip them here and save Jude's motor fix for the Emilia Motor Valley day out of Florence (Lamborghini / Ferrari / Ducati). Noting them so the choice is on the record, not an oversight.

— Weird, wacky & unforgettable

Offbeat & hidden gems

The strange, memorable, off-the-track finds the boys will actually talk about — vetted, real, and doable carless by foot, tram and metro from a base near Porta Nuova. Turin has a genuine occult-and-engineering streak; this is the good stuff around the two big hitters.

Jude & Keir · underground siege history

The Pietro Micca tunnels

Beneath the streets runs a 14-km maze of 18th-century military counter-mines — narrow stone galleries dug to blow up besieging French armies. In 1706 a young miner, Pietro Micca, lit a charge that saved the city and killed himself doing it. The museum takes you down into the real tunnels: cramped, dark, and completely gripping.

Older kids + up (tight, dark passages) · central, book a guided descent

museopietromicca.it
Jude · cars, no museum ticket

The Lingotto rooftop test track

The old Fiat Lingotto factory had cars roll off the line and straight up onto a banked test track on the roof — the one from the original Italian Job. The factory is now a mall, but the rooftop track survives as a walkable oval with a hanging "bubble" and a garden. Jude gets his car hit for the price of a metro ride, and no museum queue.

All ages · Metro to Lingotto · rooftop (Pista 500) is ticketed but cheap

pinacoteca-agnelli.it
parents + Rhys · spooky city lore

Magic Turin — black & white squares

Turin has traded on an esoteric reputation for centuries: legend puts it on both a "white magic" triangle (with Lyon and Prague) and a "black magic" one (with London and San Francisco). Piazza Statuto is said to be the dark heart, Piazza Castello the light. It's pure folklore — and a genuinely fun, free self-guided walk hunting the symbols and statues that fuel it.

All ages · free · self-guided, or book a night ghost-walk

turismotorino.org
Keir & Grey · a rack-railway up a hill

Superga by the old cog tram

A historic rack tramway from 1884 grinds up out of the city to the Basilica of Superga on its hilltop, for a huge view over Turin to the Alps and the royal tombs inside. It's also the poignant crash site of the 1949 Grande Torino football team. A short, quirky ride that feels like a mini-funicular adventure.

All ages · tram/bus to Sassi, then the rack railway · check running days

turismotorino.org
