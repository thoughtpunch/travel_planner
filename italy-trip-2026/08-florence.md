## Why Florence as the first work base

Seven nights is enough to stop touring and start living somewhere. This is where Dan's work rhythm kicks in — 4–11pm Italy time — so the days flip: **mornings and early afternoons are the family window**, evenings are quiet. ## What's in Florence itself

Family rule in effect: **one** major Renaissance gallery, not five.

### The one big-hitter (pick one)
- **Uffizi OR Accademia** — Botticelli's *Birth of Venus* + *Primavera* at the Uffizi, or Michelangelo's *David* at the Accademia. Choose one, go early, keep it tight. Under-18s free (Grey and Keir; Rhys now pays).
- Optional supplement: the **Vasari Corridor** — the Medici's private elevated tunnel, three-quarters of a kilometre from Palazzo Vecchio over the Ponte Vecchio to Pitti, so the Grand Duke never had to walk with the commoners. Sealed for eight years, reopened December 2024. Book with the Uffizi, max 5 tickets, well ahead.

### The Florence-done-our-way core
- **Duomo complex + climb the dome** — Brunelleschi's dome, 463 steps up between the two shells past the frescoes, and/or Giotto's campanile for the rival view. The single best morning in Florence. Book the timed dome slot early.
- **Stibbert Museum** — an eccentric collector's villa crammed with European armor and a full hall of Japanese samurai armor and swords. The boys' and Kei's favorite, blissfully uncrowded, worth the short ride out.
- **Museo Galileo** — astrolabes, armillary spheres, early automata, Galileo's own telescopes, and his actual finger preserved in a glass egg. Peak curiosity-cabinet energy for the parents.
- **HZERO** — an enormous, obsessively detailed model railway and miniature city (day-to-night lighting) in Palazzo Bombicci. Pure delight for Keir and Grey.
- **Palazzo Vecchio secret passages + tower** — the "Monster" hidden-passages tour behind the state rooms, then climb the Arnolfo tower over the piazza. Storytelling adventure the kids actually engage with (tour 4+).
- **Bargello** — Donatello's David and the great Renaissance sculpture in a former prison palace. Calm, uncrowded, the connoisseur's alternative to the gallery crush. Under-18s free.
- **Brancacci Chapel** — Masaccio's frescoes that launched the Renaissance, three minutes from the door, then a Santo Spirito aperitivo as the square fills up.
- **Boboli & Bardini gardens** — green space to breathe, statues and grottoes to find, plants for Kei's botany; Bardini's wisteria terrace has the best rooftop view of the city.

### Worldschool: a Florentine craft course
- **A hands-on artisan workshop** is the signature Florence day and a natural worldschool fit — make something and carry it home. **Leather at the Scuola del Cuoio**, paper-marbling / bookbinding, mosaic, or metal-embossing. Book ahead.
- **AquaFlor** — in a Renaissance palazzo near Santa Croce, a master perfumer walks you through 100+ essences to blend and bottle a fragrance that's entirely your own. Ninety proper grown-up minutes; best 12+, daily 10:30 & 15:30, book ahead. Rhys's kind of thing now that he's 18.

### Weird-medieval / Voynich for Kei and the parents
- **La Specola** — taxidermy halls and the famous 18th-century wax anatomical models. Beautiful, unsettling, newly reopened after restoration. Ten minutes from the base.
- **Officina di Santa Maria Novella** — the world's oldest pharmacy (Dominican friars brewing elixirs since 1221); frescoed sales rooms, old apothecary jars, a small museum in the workshops. Free to walk in.
- **The Buontalenti Grotto** in Boboli — a Mannerist fake cave dripping fake stalactites, sponge-stone shepherds and figures melting out of the walls, built for Francesco I in the 1580s. Included with the Boboli ticket, timed viewings.
- **Buchette del vino** — the medieval "wine windows" cut into palazzo walls, built in the 1500s so noble families could sell wine tax-free straight to the street. ~150 survive and a handful are live again; order gelato, a spritz or a Chianti through the hole. **Babae on Via Santo Spirito** pours through its buchetta near our base.
- **Zecchi** — the Renaissance color shop on Via dello Studio that still grinds pigments by hand from Cennino Cennini's 14th-century recipes, including real ultramarine from ground lapis. Where gemstones literally become paint — catnip for Jude the rockhound.
- **The window that can never be closed** — on Piazza Santissima Annunziata, one shutter of Palazzo Grifoni has stood open for nearly 500 years; legend says a bride watched from it for a husband who never came home, and the family gave up trying to close it after she died. The bronze horseman in the square stares straight at it. Free, and genuinely eerie.

## The Bologna food day-trip — 37 minutes by Frecciarossa

**Bologna is no longer an overnight stop; it's a day out of the Florence week.** **Firenze S.M.N. → Bologna Centrale in ~37 minutes** on a direct Frecciarossa, frequent departures all day, both stations central and walkable. No lodging to book. Go early — **Bologna eats best at lunch** — and be back in Florence for the evening. Buy the tickets same-week; it's a short leg with flat advance fares, not one of the dynamic-priced spine legs.

**The day, in order of priority:**

1. **A hands-on fresh-pasta class** — hand-roll the sfoglia and fold tortellini and tagliatelle, with a station for every kid. La Vecchia Scuola Bolognese, or a home class through Cesarine. Sells out; reserve early.
2. **Quadrilatero market food crawl** — the medieval market lanes off Piazza Maggiore: cured meats and tortelloni at **Tamburini**, a stand-up lunch in the **Mercato di Mezzo**, a glass at **Osteria del Sole** (a 1465 wine-only bar where they pour and you bring food in from the stalls around it).
3. **The feast** — tagliatelle al ragù (never "spaghetti bolognese," which doesn't exist here), tortellini in brodo, real mortadella. Book one proper osteria for the big sit-down lunch; it's the whole reason to make the trip.
4. **The Two Towers — climb Asinelli** — 498 wooden steps up the leaning tower for the best view over the red rooftops. Small timed slots, book ahead. Age 8+ for the climb.
5. **The porticoes** — 40 km of them, a UNESCO site; the longest, **Portico di San Luca**, climbs 666 arches to a hilltop basilica. Walk it, or ride the San Luca Express up and stroll down.
6. **Palazzo Poggi** — the university's wunderkammer: 18th-century anatomical waxes, obstetric teaching figures, ship models, maps and natural-history oddities in a frescoed palace. Adults + older kids.
7. **Archiginnasio + the Anatomical Theatre** — the old university hall, walls crusted with carved student coats-of-arms, hiding a wooden dissection theatre with tiered benches around a marble slab and skinned wooden figures watching over it.
8. **Santo Stefano's "seven churches"** — a maze of seven interlinked churches and courtyards stacked up over centuries, layering Roman columns, a Romanesque courtyard and an early replica of the Holy Sepulchre. Quiet, strange, free — with a gelato from the piazza.
9. **Optional: a Modena morning** — half an hour further by train for Parmigiano-Reggiano made at dawn and barrel-aged balsamic in family attics. Or the **Grand Tour Italia** food park at the edge of town (the old FICO; re-check opening days).
10. **Motor Valley — for Jude** — Ferrari, Lamborghini and Ducati museums are all in this corner of Italy. Honest heads-up: a factory day is its own outing. Make it a **second day-trip**, not a bolt-on to the food run.

**Bologna's offbeat corners:**
- **La Finestrella di Via Piella** — a shuttered window in a plain wall opening onto a hidden canal between the houses, the last uncovered stretch of the buried medieval waterways. Bologna's "Little Venice." Via Piella 16, free.
- **The whispering telephone** — under the vaulted Voltone del Podestà, two people at opposite diagonal corners whisper into the stone and hear each other perfectly across the noisy square. Split up, whisper a password, watch Keir's face.
- **Neptune's not-a-finger** — from one exact spot by the Sala Borsa entrance, the bronze Neptune's raised thumb turns into something distinctly ruder. A 450-year-old visual dirty joke in the main square. Rhys's.
- **The three arrows of Corte Isolani** — three ancient wooden arrows lodged in the portico ceiling at Strada Maggiore 19, with a suitably grisly legend attached.
- **Cassini's sundial in San Petronio** — a 67 m brass meridian line across the floor, the world's longest indoor one (1655), with a pinhole 27 m up dropping a disc of sunlight that crawls along it at solar noon. Be there at noon. The basilica's façade is still half bare brick because (legend says) a jealous Pope diverted the money.
- **Museo per la Memoria di Ustica** — a hushed hall rebuilt around the wreckage of a DC-9 shot down in 1980, wrapped in Boltanski's installation of 81 pulsing lights and 81 mirrors. Devastating; older boys and adults. Free, Fri–Sun 17:00–20:00, 10 min from Centrale.
- **Gelato Museum Carpigiani** — 20+ antique churning machines, the science of freezing, and hands-on workshops where you make a batch. Anzola, ~20 min by bus 87. Grey's whole trip in one building.
- **The tortellino & Venus's navel** — the legend that an innkeeper shaped the first tortellino on the goddess's navel, glimpsed through a keyhole. Order tortellini in brodo, know the story, settle the family argument after the pasta class.

## What's hard about Florence

- **September is still warm** (upper 70s–80s°F) — climb the Duomo early, apartment AC matters
- **The center is mobbed** by day — our San Frediano base and early starts are the whole strategy
- **Everything worth doing is timed-entry** — Duomo, Uffizi/Accademia, Vasari Corridor, workshops, the Bologna pasta class and Asinelli tower all need booking, not walk-up
- **Evening family time is Dan-light** on work days — front-load him into mornings and the arrival weekend
- **Cobblestones + bridges** — good walking shoes; it's flat but you'll cross the river a lot
- **Seven people, not six, this week** — Grandma is with us, so table bookings and day-trip tickets need the extra seat
