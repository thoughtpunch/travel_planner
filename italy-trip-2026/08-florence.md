## Why Florence as the first work base

Seven nights is enough to stop touring and start living somewhere. This is where Dan's work rhythm kicks in — 4–11pm Italy time — so the days flip: **mornings and early afternoons are the family window**, evenings are quiet. That shape actually suits Florence perfectly. Climb the Duomo before the heat, do a hands-on workshop after lunch, and the Renaissance is right outside the door.

The family rule here: **pick ONE great art experience, not five galleries.** Spend the rest of the week on armor, automata, model railways, artisan workshops and a Tuscan day trip. EU under-18s are free at every Italian state museum — a real saver with Grey (12), Keir (9), and Rhys (free until Sep 18).

## What's in Florence itself

Family rule in effect: **one** major Renaissance gallery, not five.

### The one big-hitter (pick one)
- **Uffizi OR Accademia** — Botticelli's *Birth of Venus* + *Primavera* at the Uffizi, or Michelangelo's *David* at the Accademia. Choose one, go early, keep it tight. Under-18s free. **Time the Uffizi around Sep 18** — the last day all four boys are free under-18 (Rhys turns 18 that evening).
- Optional supplement: the **Vasari Corridor** — the Medici's private elevated tunnel over the Ponte Vecchio, reopened Dec 2024. Book with the Uffizi, max 5 tickets, well ahead.

### The Florence-done-our-way core
- **Duomo complex + climb the dome** — Brunelleschi's dome, 463 steps up between the two shells past the frescoes, and/or Giotto's campanile. The single best morning in Florence. Book the timed dome slot early.
- **Stibbert Museum** — an eccentric collector's villa crammed with European armor and a full hall of Japanese samurai armor and swords. The boys' and Kei's favorite, blissfully uncrowded, worth the short ride out.
- **Museo Galileo** — astrolabes, armillary spheres, early automata, Galileo's own telescopes, and his actual finger preserved in a glass egg. Peak curiosity-cabinet energy for the parents.
- **HZERO** — an enormous, obsessively detailed model railway and miniature city (day-to-night lighting) in Palazzo Bombicci. Pure delight for Keir and Grey.
- **Palazzo Vecchio secret passages + tower** — the "Monster" hidden-passages tour, then climb the Arnolfo tower. Storytelling adventure the kids actually engage with.

### Worldschool: a Florentine craft course
- **A hands-on artisan workshop** is the signature Florence day and a natural worldschool fit — make something and carry it home. **Leather at the Scuola del Cuoio**, paper-marbling / bookbinding, mosaic, or metal-embossing. Book ahead. (Doubles as Rhys's grown-up-skill birthday activity.)

### Weird-medieval / Voynich for Kei and the parents
- **La Specola** — taxidermy halls and the famous 18th-century wax anatomical models. Beautiful, unsettling, newly reopened after restoration.
- **Officina di Santa Maria Novella** — the world's oldest pharmacy (friars brewing elixirs since 1221); frescoed sales rooms and old apothecary jars. Free to walk in.
- **The Buontalenti Grotto** in Boboli — a Mannerist fake cave dripping fake stalactites and melting figures. Included with the Boboli ticket.
- **Buchette del vino** — the medieval "wine windows" cut into palazzo walls; order gelato, a spritz, or (for Rhys) a Chianti handed through the hole. Babae on Via Santo Spirito pours through its buchetta near our base.
- **Zecchi** — the Renaissance color shop that still grinds pigments by hand (real ultramarine from ground lapis) — catnip for Jude the rockhound.

## What's hard about Florence

- **September is still warm** (upper 70s–80s°F) — climb the Duomo early, apartment AC matters
- **The center is mobbed** by day — our Oltrarno base and early starts are the whole strategy
- **Everything worth doing is timed-entry** — Duomo, Uffizi/Accademia, Vasari Corridor, workshops all need booking, not walk-up
- **Evening family time is Dan-light** on work days — front-load him into mornings and the arrival-day birthday
- **Cobblestones + bridges** — good walking shoes; it's flat but you'll cross the river a lot
