## The trip in one paragraph

A 34-night family rail loop through Italy — **Milan back to Milan**, September 11 to October 15, 2026 — for a family of six. **Eight stops**: Milan, Turin, the Ligurian coast (based in Chiavari), Florence, Rome, Venice/Lido, the Dolomites, and a last night near Malpensa. Everything moves by train except the final mountain leg and the run home, which are one **booked** 5-day one-way rental car (Budget, VCE Oct 10 → MXP Oct 15). Dan keeps working remote 4–11pm local Italian time, five days a week, from the two long work bases: **Florence (7 nights)** and **Rome (7 nights)**. Worldschool for the four boys in the mornings. Flights are booked and paid ($5,346) and sit outside the in-country budget. Three milestones land inside the five weeks: Rhys turns 18 on the coast, the anniversary in Venice, and Grey turns 12 in the Dolomites.

## Housing strategy

Three rules, from `/housing`:

1. **Whole place, always.** One apartment that sleeps all six *in its bedrooms* — never a clutch of hotel rooms. Two bedrooms is acceptable if the second holds bunks; a living-room sofa bed does not count, because that room is Dan's office from 4pm.
2. **Belt over center.** The bigger, quieter, cheaper apartment one stop out that's still on the line — Oltrarno not the Duomo scrum, the Lido not San Marco.
3. **On the line, or don't book it.** Every base walkable to rail, ferry, funicular or metro. Turin by Porta Nuova, not a hill town; Chiavari on the main-line coast, not a Cinque Terre cliff village. The only exception is the Dolomites, where we have the car.

The **weekly rate** kicks in at 7 nights, so **Florence, Rome and Venice/Lido** all clear it; Chiavari (4n) and Kastelruth (4n) get part of the way. On the 2-night legs there's no rate to chase, so optimize for position instead. The Dolomites base is the one leg no train reaches — and it came in **$396 under estimate** at $1,004. Total lodging lands **€8–10K** across the 34 nights.

### The Dolomites squeeze — worth knowing

Four nights is **only three full days: Oct 11, 12, 13.** Oct 10 is a travel afternoon (boat, airport, car, drive) and Oct 14 is the run to Malpensa. Oct 12 is locked to Grey's birthday. That leaves Oct 11 and Oct 13 for Keir's Kronplatz bike-park day and Jude's rocks-and-minerals day — which means **Alpe di Siusi has no slot left.** It's either cut or squeezed into the Oct 10 arrival afternoon if the drive goes quickly. One useful wrinkle: the Siusi→Compatsch road is closed to private cars 09:00–17:00, but **reopens after 17:00**, which makes a sunset dinner up at Compatsch (~1,850 m) possible on the car leg — sunset mid-October is ~18:30.

On Dan's work rhythm, that leg breaks down as: **Sat Oct 10 and Sun Oct 11 free** (weekend), **Mon Oct 12 must be blocked** for Grey's birthday, **Tue Oct 13** is the only normal work evening so keep that day's plan close to base, and **Wed Oct 14 and Thu Oct 15 both need PTO** (driving through the work window, then flying).
