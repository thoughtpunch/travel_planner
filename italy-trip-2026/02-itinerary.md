## The travel legs (in order)

Five rail legs, then a boat-and-car finish. Fares are the cheapest advance pulls from Aug 2026,
all six of us, Famiglia discount applied where it works.

1. **Milan → Turin** — Frecciarossa / Italo, Milano Centrale → Torino Porta Nuova, ~1h (**Sun Sep 13**) · from €77
2. **Turin → Chiavari via Genoa** — Torino P.N. → Genova P. Principe ~1h45, **submarine + sea-museum stop in Genoa**, then regionale Genova → Chiavari ~40 min (**Tue Sep 15**, PTO) · from €91
3. **Chiavari → Florence** — Regionale Veloce / Intercity → Firenze S.M.N., ~2h30 (**Sat Sep 19**) · from €118
4. **Florence → Rome** — Frecciarossa / Italo, ~1h35 (**Sat Sep 26**) · from €99
5. **Rome → Venice** — Frecciarossa / Italo, Roma Termini → Venezia S. Lucia direct ~4h, then vaporetto to the Lido (**Sat Oct 3**) · from €176
6. **Lido → Venice Marco Polo (VCE), collect the car → Val Gardena** (**Sat Oct 10**) — **Alilaguna boat straight from the Lido to the airport.** No train, no hauling six people's bags through S. Lucia. *This replaces the old €152–180 Venezia S. Lucia → Verona → Bolzano rail leg, which is dead.*
7. **Ortisei → the Malpensa area, by car** (**Wed Oct 14**) — ~370 km, ~4h30, motorway the whole way, **lunch stop at Sirmione on Lake Garda**. A driving/sightseeing day, not a transfer sprint: we keep the car overnight and **drop it one-way at MXP Terminal 1 at 12:00 on Thu Oct 15**, four hours before the flight. *This replaces the old €180–290 Bolzano → Verona → Milano Centrale → Malpensa Express rail leg, also dead.*

The **fast dynamic-priced legs (1, 4, 5)** are priced like flights — book the moment fares open,
~4 months out. See [/trains](public/trains.html) for the family-discount split: two bookings of
three, each carrying an under-15 (Grey and Keir), since Offerta Famiglia caps at five people.

## Why this shape

- **Milan first** — the flights land there. Icelandair touches down at MXP at 9:55pm Fri Sep 11,
  so the first two nights are a soft landing before the loop starts moving.
- **Turin instead of Lake Como.** The western swing buys an under-touristed café-and-arcade city
  with a mountaintop abbey and the Mole's glass elevator, and it feeds naturally down to Genoa
  and the coast. Como was a dead-end detour that would have doubled back through Milan.
- **Chiavari, not Levanto or a Cinque Terre village.** A real working town with its own beach and
  a main-line station dead-center on the Tigullio gulf: ~25 min west to Camogli, ~10 min to
  Sestri Levante, ~40+ min east to the five villages. Four nights of sea and rest, all by train.
- **Florence + Rome are the two 7-night work bases.** The only stops long enough to unlock a
  weekly lodging rate, and where Dan settles into his evening work rhythm. Everything before them
  is a moving vacation front; everything after is the loop home.
- **Bologna is a lunch, not a bed.** 37 minutes from the Florence base means tortellini,
  the Quadrilatero and the porticoes — and home in time for Dan's 16:00 window. An overnight
  bought nothing but two moving days. Jude's Motor Valley (Lamborghini/Ferrari/Ducati) is the
  optional add-on from Bologna.
- **Seven nights on the Lido, because MuMu is the point.** Visiting Aunt Muriel is a reason this
  trip exists, not an add-on. Seven unhurried nights leave room for long lunches, a Padua day,
  and the anniversary dinner on Oct 9 without ever rushing her.
- **Carless except the Dolomites and the run home.** Every base is walkable to a station,
  vaporetto or metro. The mountains are the one leg that genuinely needs wheels — and by taking
  the car at VCE and giving it back at MXP on flight day, the car *is* the transfer at both ends
  instead of an extra errand bolted onto two rail journeys. Holding it the extra night also buys
  a relaxed Oct 14 drive and a mobile Oct 15 morning.

## The Dolomites four-night squeeze

Four nights is **only three full days: Oct 11, 12, 13.** Oct 10 is a travel afternoon (boat to
VCE, collect the car, drive up), Oct 14 is the drive to Malpensa. Oct 12 is locked to Grey's
birthday. That leaves exactly two open days for everything else:

```
Sat Oct 10  Alilaguna Lido → VCE, collect the 7-seater, drive to Ortisei.
            Travel afternoon. The ONLY possible slot for Alpe di Siusi —
            and only if the drive goes quickly.
Sun Oct 11  Open day → Kronplatz Bike Park (Keir) OR Jude's rocks day
Mon Oct 12  LOCKED — Grey's 12th. Seceda, rifugio lunch, Ortisei pizza night
Tue Oct 13  Open day → whichever of Kronplatz / Jude's rocks day is left
Wed Oct 14  Drive Ortisei → Sirmione (lunch on Garda) → the Malpensa area,
            ~370 km / 4h30. Sleep near MXP with the car (location TBD).
Thu Oct 15  Morning still has the car → drop it at MXP T1 12:00 → fly 16:20
```

- **Kronplatz Bike Park (Keir)** — 18 trails off the Olang gondola, skills parks, rentals and a
  bike school on site; open to 8 Nov. It's ~1h30 from Ortisei up the Puster valley, which only
  works *because this is the one leg with a car*. Book kids' bike sizes ahead.
- **Jude's rocks day** — the **Ridnaun / Monteneve mining museum**, one of the highest mines in
  Europe: helmet and lamp, 200 m into a real ore tunnel, Victorian ore-crushers running. Open to
  Oct 31, Tue–Sun. Book the Ridnaun site — the full ride-the-train-900 m-in Monteneve tour winds
  down mid-Oct.
- **Alpe di Siusi has no slot left.** It is either cut or squeezed into the Oct 10 arrival
  afternoon. Note the road rule: the Siusi → Compatsch road is **closed to private cars
  09:00–17:00** year-round while the cableway runs, so by day you go up on the Mont Sëuc cableway
  from Ortisei. **It reopens to cars after 17:00** — which makes a sunset dinner up at Compatsch
  (~1,850 m) possible on the car leg. Mid-October sunset is ~18:30.

**Dan's work window on this leg:** Sat Oct 10 and Sun Oct 11 are free. **Mon Oct 12 must be
blocked** for Grey's birthday. **Tue Oct 13 is the only normal work evening** — keep that day's
plan close to base. **Wed Oct 14 and Thu Oct 15 both need PTO** (driving through the window,
then flying).

## Worldschool rhythm (default daily shape)

Dan works **16:00–23:00 Italian local time, Mon–Fri**. Mornings and early afternoons are wide
open. This rhythm applies mainly at the **two work bases — Florence (Sep 19–26) and Rome
(Sep 26–Oct 3)** — where the family is settled in one apartment for a full week. The shorter
stops (Milan, Turin, Chiavari, Lido, Dolomites) are lighter on work and run closer to full
vacation days, and the first week is PTO outright.

```
07:30–09:30  Breakfast at the apartment, slow start
09:30–12:30  Worldschool block + family activity
12:30–14:00  Lunch — light, often at home or a market
14:00–16:00  Family activity, day-trip core, museum, gelato
16:00–23:00  Dan working from the apartment (mostly calls)
18:30–20:00  Early family dinner OR Dan steps out at ~8pm to join
23:00–00:00  Dan done — late family time, bed
```

Weekday day trips — the Bologna food run from Florence, Lucca/Pisa, Padua from the Lido — need
to wrap by ~15:30 so Dan can be at his desk by 16:00. Weekends are wide open, which is why the
intercity moves land on Saturdays and Sundays (the one midweek move, Turin → Chiavari on Tue
Sep 15, sits inside the PTO week). Italian dinner culture (20:00–22:00) fits well: the family
can eat early together (~18:30) or late with Dan after his shift.

Lodging follows from this: every apartment must sleep all six **in its bedrooms**. A living-room
sofa bed does not count, because that room is Dan's office from 16:00 to 23:00.
